## 1. Objective

Finish the phase on the chart path. PH-38.3 made every **tick** a read route
returns carry the frame its integer counts in; a candle is four integers from
the same lattice and carries nothing, so `GET /markets/:id/history` is the last
route that hands a broker a number it cannot interpret.

## 2. Why a candle is decidable and not a guess

The candle store keeps `first_sequence` and `last_sequence` on every row
(`packages/runtime/src/sqliteHistory.ts`), and a rolled-up bar keeps them too —
a 1d bar's first and last are the first and last of the minutes it was folded
from. The record's frame log is keyed by sequence. So a bar's frame is
`frameAtOrBefore(epochs, firstSequence)`, exactly, and the question "does this
bar span two frames?" is answered by `frameOfSpan`, which asks whether any epoch
**begins inside** the span. No heuristic, no instant arithmetic.

**This paragraph described comparing the two ends** (Cycle Audit 13, a3-05), and
the code says in bold that comparing the ends is not that test: a 1h `aix-idx`
bar read 5.16% off with both of its ends agreeing, because an epoch that begins
and ends inside one bar leaves the ends equal. The record described the defect
rather than the fix.

The lookup is done once per request, not once per bar: an asset has a handful
of epochs, the window may hold twenty thousand bars, and the resolution is an
array scan over the handful.

## 3. What a straddling bar gets, and why not a hole

A bar whose minutes were published under two different lattices has an open in
one unit and a close in the other, and a high and low that are the extremes of a
mixture. Folding produced four integers that are **not prices in either frame**.

Two honest answers were available: withhold the bar, or return it and refuse to
say what it counts in. This takes the second, for the same reason PH-38.3 takes
it for a tick: the integers are what the venue stored and they are not in doubt;
what is in doubt is their interpretation, and a null says so. Withholding would
put a hole in a chart where the venue does hold data, and a hole is a worse lie
than an admission — a client cannot tell it from an outage.

So a candle carries `logQuantum` and `referencePrice`, both **null** when its
first and last sequences resolve to different frames, or when either is
undeclared.

## 4. What is built

- `GET /markets/:id/history` resolves the asset's epochs once and stamps each
  candle with the frame it counts in, or nulls.
- The candle item gains those two keys.

  **What this bullet claimed about the contract is false, and the reason matters
  more than the claim** (Cycle Audit 13, a3-03, confirmed clause by clause by a
  refuter). No version moved: the contract is still `3.0.0` with digest
  `d205f4f674c19cfa`, there is no `3.1.0` entry in `CONTRACT_HISTORY`, and the
  digest **cannot** move for a change of this kind, because `candles` is typed
  `'array'` and the digest hashes the route shapes. The candle item — the thing a
  chart consumes and the thing this subphase changed — has no shape in the
  contract, no version, and no exported type: `venueClient` hands a broker
  `unknown[]`. A refuter fed the route `[{nonsense:true,logQuantum:'banana'}, 7,
null]` and the shape check passed it, and the conformance suite never requests
  `/markets/:id/history` at all. Typing the candle item and versioning it is
  carried as its own work in the audit's record; what this subphase actually did
  is add two keys no client can discover.

- The panel draws each bar on the frame the bar states, instead of on the
  catalogue's current one. That is the visible half, and it is the half a
  broker's own chart will copy from the guide.

## 5. Guards, each watched failing

- A bar published under one frame states it; a bar folded across a boundary
  states neither. Built on a real `SqliteCandleHistory` and a real record whose
  frame log has two epochs, with the bar's `firstSequence`/`lastSequence`
  straddling the boundary.
- An asset with no declared frame states neither, rather than the current one.
- The resolution is per bar and not per request: a window holding bars from both
  sides of a boundary gets different answers within one response.

## 6. What this does not do

It does not convert or rewrite a stored candle, for the reason the phase
document gives in §3 — and here with less at stake, since a candle is
presentation-derived and `settle()` never reads one. It does not add a
`displayOpen`/`displayClose` string: the venue already ships the portable
conversion, and a broker that has the frame has the price.
