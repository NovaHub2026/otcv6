# PH-21.2 — The Venue And The Store Under A Full Catalogue

Status: APPROVED

## 1. Objective

Answer questions 3 and 4 of PH-21 §1 with an executed measurement rather than
an argument: **what does the venue cost per tick with a hundred markets**, and
**what does a quarter of history cost on disk**.

Both were reasoned rather than measured. The venue's advance loops every market
and the markets share a clock and nothing else, so the cost _should_ be linear
— PH-21's own words, with the "should" doing the work. And the whole case for a
hundred-asset catalogue rests on one arithmetic claim in
`CATALOGUE_AND_PANEL.md` §4: 131,759 candles per asset per quarter, because the
count is fixed by the calendar rather than by the tick rate. Nobody had
multiplied it by a hundred and weighed the result.

## 2. Scope

- `tools/sim/src/venueScale.ts` — a deliberate act, not a test. It builds the
  personalities once at the largest size and reuses them across sizes, because
  what is being measured is the runtime and a fresh solve per size would measure
  the solve. Scheduling at 5, 25, 50 and 100 markets through six simulated hours
  in steps of the catch-up bound; storage from a real `SqliteCandleHistory`
  backfilled with eight assets over two days.
- `docs/evidence/CYCLE-7-VENUE-SCALE.md` — the recorded run.

**Excluded:** the publication and history paths that consume the venue's output
(they are downstream of the loop this measures, and named in the evidence as
unmeasured); the store under concurrent readers, which belongs to the multi-node
contract and to Issue #9.

## 3. What the measurement found

**Scheduling is linear and cheap.** 26.8 µs per market-advance at five markets,
55.7 µs at a hundred; twenty times the markets for twenty times the wall clock,
with throughput falling 7% across the range. A hundred real-time markets cost on
the order of 0.04% of one core to generate. Generation is not what will limit
this catalogue.

**Storage is what the arithmetic promised.** 52 bytes per minute bar, 75 kB per
asset-day, 6.7 MB per asset-quarter, **0.67 GB for a hundred assets over a
quarter**. `CATALOGUE_AND_PANEL.md` §4's 129,599 minute bars at 52 bytes is
6.74 MB, so the claim the catalogue's affordability rests on is now a
measurement rather than a multiplication.

## 4. The instrument was wrong first, and that is the part worth keeping

The runner's first execution reported **0.0 MB on disk** for 23,032 bars it had
certainly written, and a hundred-asset quarter at **0.00 GB**. It called `stat`
on `history.db` immediately after the writes; SQLite in WAL mode had everything
in `history.db-wal`, and the file being measured was nearly empty.

Two things about that are worth more than the fix.

**The wrong number was pleasant.** This project's case for a large catalogue is
that storage is cheap, and "storage is free" is that case, only better. A wrong
number pointing the way the author already believes is the one that survives
being read.

**What caught it was a column the table did not have.** 0.0 MB is not obviously
absurd for a hundred assets when you already expect the answer to be small; 0
bytes per bar is obviously absurd for a database row. The runner reports bytes
per bar now, so the next wrong total is visible in the table rather than in the
total alone — and it closes the database and sums `history.db`, `-wal` and
`-shm`, so neither a checkpoint that has happened nor one that has not can move
the figure.

## 5. Acceptance criteria

1. The scheduling cost per market-advance is measured at four sizes spanning
   five to a hundred markets and is linear in markets within the noise of the
   run. **Met**: 26.8 / 52.9 / 57.9 / 55.7 µs.
2. The storage cost per asset-quarter is measured from a real store, and agrees
   with the count `CATALOGUE_AND_PANEL.md` §4 derives from the calendar.
   **Met**: 6.7 MB measured against 6.74 MB derived.
3. The run is reproducible from the repository, seeded, and taken on a machine
   with no other load. **Met**: `OTC_VENUE_SEED=venue-scale`; the demo engine
   and panel were stopped for it, because CA6-01 is this project's record of
   what a contended wall-clock measurement is worth.
