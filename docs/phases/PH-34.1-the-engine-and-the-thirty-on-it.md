## 1. Objective

Build what PH-34 §2 decided, in the engine, and regenerate the catalogue of
thirty on it: regimes as a ladder that lasts like a market, at levels against
the real instrument's typical volatility; a floor under the level; ticks that
follow the regime; each asset's tick rate set by its character and 40% lower
across the catalogue.

## 2. What is built

**The regime layer** (`regime.ts`). `REGIME_LEVELS` (1.2, 1.5, 2.2, 3.5 of the
real typical level), `REGIME_LADDER` (one step at a time), `REGIME_DURATIONS`
(minimums 30/45/20/10 minutes plus an exponential remainder, medians about
1.4 h, 2 h, 50 min, 18 min). `splitRegimeLevel` divides a level between tick
rate and size so that variance per unit time is `level²` at any duration
coupling and under the rate floor. The modulator reports its `activity`.

**Arrivals follow it** (`hawkes.ts`, `factory.ts`). The regime's activity
scales the Hawkes immigrant rate and never the branching: scaling the whole
process would have multiplied the branching ratio, and a stressed regime at
×2.3 on a market at 0.6 would have been explosive at 1.4.

**The floor** (`floor.ts`). The product of the regime's level, the cascade
against its typical state and the structure phase never falls below calm; the
modulator lifts a tick by exactly the shortfall. Stateless and last in the
stack; a multiplier, so the calibration's exact rescale still holds
(`floor.test.ts` doubles the base and watches every tick double).

**Personality** (`personality.ts`). `regimeTempo` maps onto a duration factor
in `[0.7, 1.5]` that never shortens a minimum; `regimeSpread` widens only the
levels above normal, through its square root; the tick-rate floor enters as a
minimum activity. `layeredInflation` enumerates the three layers jointly with
the floor, per tick, and the clustering solve bisects against it;
`otcDispersionFactor` is how much more than the real instrument an asset
moves, typical against typical.

**Tempo by character** (`families.ts`, `seats.ts`). An archetype's or a seat's
tempo range is derived from its dispersion and burstiness
(`TICK_RATE_PER_ROOT_DISPERSION`, a square-root law normalised to 2.47 ticks a
second across the thirty), and a draw derives its tempo from its budget.

**Registration** (`registration.ts`). A request's `dispersion` is the real
instrument's; the budget is that times `otcDispersionFactor`. A request's
`meanIntervalMs` is **fitted by measurement**: the Hawkes formula
`tempo · (1 − burstiness)` was off by ×1.08–×3.0 on the catalogue, furthest on
the burstiest assets, so the calibration's measured interval corrects the tempo
and reruns until they agree within 5%.

**The runtime** folds `ENGINE_MODEL` into every checkpoint fingerprint, so a
checkpoint of the old model seams instead of resuming under the new one.

**The catalogue** is regenerated (`npm run catalogue:build -- --replicates 3`,
4.2 minutes), **keeping every recorded lattice**: a running market's prices are
integers on it, and a new quantum would move the shown price at the upgrade
(three assets would also have changed display precision).

## 3. What it measured

|                                             |                 Before |                    After |
| ------------------------------------------- | ---------------------: | -----------------------: |
| Mean tick rate per asset                    |                4.12 /s |           2.47 /s (−40%) |
| Calmest / fastest asset                     | EUR/GBP 1.9, DOGE 10.8 |  EUR/GBP 0.92, DOGE 4.22 |
| Rate against its target                     |                      — | within ±2% on all thirty |
| Median stressed episode, typical asset      |                  5 min |  ≈18 min, never under 10 |
| Default engine, excess kurtosis (10M ticks) |              62.3 (1M) |                     18.6 |

- The mirror test passes on the full stack, every layer active.
- Excess kurtosis falls because the floor removes the low tail of the level.
  Three shallow archetypes had their band floors lowered to what their
  cascades reach above it (cross-fx 85 → 35, energy 100 → 45, alt-crypto
  130 → 65); GBP/JPY and DOGE retreated one step each in the build.

## 4. What it found that was not about the tempo

The Lab's parity adjustment took the neighbour on the side of the move from
the current price, so a "win by minimum distance" on an up position could be
armed at its own entry — a tie — while the row said "win". A preset now
adjusts only towards the side that keeps its outcome, and a tie is not
adjusted at all. `closeRoutes.test.ts` found it on the new realisation; the
epoch-0 market had never produced the configuration.

## 5. Approved from evidence

The gate above ran on `ed7064f`, the tip of this subphase, with nothing edited
under it. Twenty assertions in ten statistical files moved with the
recalibration and every one was re-measured by its own procedure rather than
retuned — the tie rates by `evidence:ties` over twelve replicates per asset,
the detection floors by the acceptance run itself, the candle-granularity
bands by the thirty assets' own candles, the design-effect control by doubling
a run whose signal scales with the tie count. The first gate's failures and
what each re-measurement found are in the commit that closed them
(`test(stat): every statistical band the recalibration moved, re-measured`).

Three claims the phase could have broken and did not: the mirror test passes
on the full stack, the battery's verdict is clean at a resolution finer than
the payout margin (0.223pp against 0.2513pp), and the thirty are still
measurably different markets (21.3–24.3% against a control at 4.7–7.8%).

## 6. Tests that leaned on the old tempo

Rewritten to assert the mechanism, not a speed: settlement waits for the first
tick after expiry rather than a fixed ten seconds; a seam's first tick is
awaited rather than assumed inside five seconds; a publish refusal is provoked
by a pass that publishes; a Lab quality sample is sized to the market time it
was sized to before. Every pinned number that moved was re-measured by its
test's own procedure and the measurement recorded beside it.
