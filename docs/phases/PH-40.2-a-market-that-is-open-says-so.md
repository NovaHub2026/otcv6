# PH-40.2 — A Market That Is Open Says So: A Heartbeat Carrying The Price In Force

Type: SUBPHASE TECHNICAL DOCUMENT
Identifier: PH-40.2
Parent: [PH-40](PH-40-the-engine-a-broker-settles-against.md)
Status: PLANNED
Created: 2026-09-27

---

## 1. Objective

The broker this phase serves refuses to open a contract on a quote older than
fifteen seconds unless a heartbeat refreshes it. The engine's stream carried only
ticks, so a quiet stretch — and a seam, during which the price in force is the
last tick before it (ADR-0021) — read to that broker as a closed market, which
is exactly what the Human Owner ruled out: _"mientras esté activo y disponible
para operar, el usuario puede hacerlo en cualquier momento"_. Give the stream a
heartbeat that keeps an open market open, and make it impossible for that
heartbeat to state a price the record will not stand behind.

## 2. The one thing a heartbeat must not say

The obvious heartbeat is "the price is still X, as of now". It is wrong on this
engine, measurably:

- A host that withholds the CPU for less than the 15 s catch-up bound is caught
  up on the next pass by publishing the missed ticks **with past instants**. A
  heartbeat that said "X as of now" during those seconds would be contradicted by
  the record a moment later — and a broker that settled or opened on it would be
  holding a price the engine never had in force.
- The engine draws the next tick before its instant falls due and holds it
  (Cycle Audit 10, a1-03). Anything keyed to the clock rather than to what has
  been published is one careless line away from telling a client about that tick.

So the heartbeat states **`asOf` = how far this market has been published and
recorded** — the clock reading of the last pass that advanced it cleanly, set
only once that pass's ticks are in the record and offered to the feed. At or
before `asOf` the price in force is final; past it, nothing is claimed.

## 4. It never runs ahead of the connection it is written to

A client still being handed earlier ticks — a replay, a burst in flight — would
otherwise be told a price it has not been shown, and a broker refreshing its
quote from the heartbeat would jump forward and then back. So a frame is owed
only when the last tick the connection delivered _is_ the one in force, or when
it has delivered none yet: a live join, which the feed continues after the tick
in force. The feed delivers synchronously inside `publish`, and `asOf` is set
after it in the same synchronous stretch, so a live connection always has the
tick in force by the time `asOf` names it.

## 5. Guards, each watched failing

| Plant                                                  | What went red                                                                                                                            |
| ------------------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `priceInForce` reports the wall clock as `asOf`        | `is the last clean pass, never the wall clock`; `holds still through an outage until the reopened market publishes, then names the seam` |
| `asOf` advances for a market stalled by name           | `holds still through an outage …` — on the slowest asset, in a pass one reopening later where the market published nothing               |
| `heartbeatOwed` ignores what the connection was handed | `is not owed to a connection still being handed earlier ticks, or into a socket that said stop`                                          |
| `/price` bound back to the last tick                   | `lets /price answer every instant up to it, and refuses the next millisecond`                                                            |
| the conformance check neutralised                      | the three fault rows: `asOf` past what the venue will price, the wrong tick, no heartbeat                                                |

**One plant survived first, and it was right to.** A separate "reopened and not
yet published" skip was planted away and nothing went red: PH-39 keeps a reopened
market stalled by name until it publishes, so the `stalled` check already covers
that window. The line was removed as redundant, and the `stalled` check was then
planted away instead — which the outage test catches, once it runs on the slowest
asset. On the fastest one the reopened market published inside the first
millisecond and the window never existed: blind data, fixed by choosing the
asset the window is real on.

## 6. What this does not do

- **A heartbeat while the process is starved.** A host that withholds the CPU
  withholds it from the timer too. What the heartbeat guarantees is that the
  first frame after the host returns says only what the record stands behind.
- **Displayed precision on the frame.** The heartbeat carries the canonical
  integer, as every stream frame does; `displayPrice` and the lattice a broker
  displays are PH-40.3 and PH-40.4.
