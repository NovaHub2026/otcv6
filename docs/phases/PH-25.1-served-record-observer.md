Identifier: PH-25.1

---

## 1. Objective

Give `packages/lab` a source it has never had: the ticks as a browser would
hold them, read over `GET /markets/:id/stream` from a running `apps/api`, and
nothing else — no engine, no snapshot, no state directory.

## 2. What is built

**`servedRecord(baseUrl, assetId, options)`** in `packages/lab` — an SSE
client for Node that connects, resumes from a sequence, reads until a target
sequence or instant, keeps every `tick` frame, records every `gap` and `close`
frame it is told, and returns a `TickSource` the existing `buildObserverDataset`
consumes unchanged. Two things it must be, and both are asserted against a
spawned service:

1. **Faithful.** Two observers on two connections produce byte-identical
   datasets (INV-002), and a dataset read after a deliberate restart of the
   service — checkpoint, SIGKILL, resume — is the same sequence, with the seam
   the record declares where the record declares it (INV-008).
2. **Honest about what it did not see.** A `gap` frame is not a tick; a
   resume the server refuses is not a continuation. The source reports both,
   and a dataset built across either says so rather than closing the hole.

**Nothing the observer cannot read.** The client uses the public read surface
only; `labSurface.test.ts`'s scan is extended so that nothing under
`packages/lab/src/served/` imports from `@otc/engine`'s generation code or from
`@otc/runtime`.

## 3. Acceptance criteria

1. A dataset built from the served record of a spawned service equals, tick for
   tick, the dataset built from the same engine in-process for the same span.
2. Two connections, identical datasets.
3. Across a restart, the dataset continues and the seam is where the record
   says.
4. A told gap is recorded, never filled.
5. The observer imports nothing but the public surface; the scan is watched
   failing against a planted engine import.

## 5. What the observer found on its first runs

Three runs of the socket half were needed, and two of the three failures were
the venue's, not the test's. They are the subphase's result.

**Run 1 — the test's own.** The stored candle and the folded candle differed
only in `firstSequence`/`lastSequence`: the observer dataset numbers the ticks
it holds from one (it keeps prices and instants, not sequences). The
comparison now checks the fold's shape and the sequences against the wire's
own ticks.

**Run 2 — the replay window does not survive a restart (finding a).** An
observer held sequences 4–1803; the venue checkpointed at 1898, was killed,
and resumed at 1898. Asking for 1804 was refused: _"older than the retained
window, which starts at 1899"_. Nothing was evicted — the shipped service
persists checkpoints and candles, not ticks, so its feed is process-local and
a restart forgets everything published before the resume point. The ticks
1804–1898 are still in the candle record (asserted: stored 1m candles span
them), reproducible from the earlier checkpoint (INV-009), and unservable over
the stream. The multi-node store (`SqliteStateStore.readRecord`) already holds
a per-tick record with seams; the shipped service does not use it. **Not fixed
here** — priming the feed from a persisted record is a phase-sized change and
goes to PH-27.5's report. The test asserts the honest behaviour: the refusal
names the window start, exactly the resume point plus one.

**Run 3 — `onGap=live` joined at the live edge, past ticks it retained
(finding b).** Told the gap, the same observer was joined at sequence 1908,
while the venue's window began at 1899: nine retained ticks never served,
inside a hole the gap frame said began at 1804. **Fixed here**, in both stream
endpoints: after the `gap` frame the subscription replays from the oldest
retained sequence (the live edge only when the refusal was the process's
replay budget), the frame carries `resumesAt`, and the gap is written before
the replay so a hole is never told after the ticks that follow it. Guarded in
`adminSurface.test.ts` (single and multiplexed), watched failing against the
previous order and against a frame without `resumesAt`.

**Run 4 — a kill costs the candle record the minute it fell in (finding c).**
With finding b fixed, the observer was joined exactly at the window start and
the hole was bounded exactly; the test then looked for the hole in the stored
candles and found **zero** 1m bars touching it; the fifth run found one bar
overlapping it partially. The minute the kill falls in is open in the recorder
and dies with the process — only closed bars are flushed — and after the
resume the recorder sees that minute from inside and withholds it (CA6-30
forbids a bar folded from a partial view). So a SIGKILL leaves the candle
record a hole of up to one minute, depending on where in the minute it fell:
honest at the minute tier, since no bar claims what it did not fold (asserted
on every bar read) — and, since Cycle Audit 9 (a6-01), at the hourly tier too:
the hour around the hole used to be folded from the minutes on either side
and stored whole, its high and low blind to the withheld ticks; `refreshRollup`
withholds an hour whose minutes do not touch in sequence now —
but a hole a chart shows. The test measures and prints it rather than
asserting a number that depends on the kill's phase. The ticks are
reproducible from the earlier checkpoint (INV-009) and stored nowhere.
**Not fixed here** — the fix is the same as finding a's, a persisted tick
record the recorder can refold from — and it is measured by the test and
carried to PH-27.5's report with a.
