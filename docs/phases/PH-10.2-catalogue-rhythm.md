## 1. Objective

Re-author the five catalogue assets so their volatility dynamics differ in time
structure, recalibrate their lattices to the process that results, and measure
what it did to scale-free differentiation.

## 2. The one thing that must not be true at the end

That the number moved because the assets got further apart in **amplitude**.

Separating EUR/USD from BTC by how far they move is trivial, true by
construction, and already what the full-signature classifier does at near 100%.
If PH-10.2 raises shape differentiation by quietly widening the volatility
spread, it will have measured nothing and will have said it measured something.

So the change is constrained: **only the five rhythm traits move.** `tempoMs`,
`burstiness`, `regimeSpread`, `structureSpread` and `durationCoupling` are
carried across from the approved catalogue unchanged, and each asset's realised
tick amplitude is held fixed by construction.

That last one needs machinery, because it is not automatic. Cascade components
have mean 1 but `E[M²] = 1 + c²`, so a deeper cascade produces larger typical
moves from the same `volatility` trait. Changing an asset's rhythm would
therefore change its amplitude as a side effect, in the exact direction that
would flatter this subphase's headline number. `authorPersonality` divides
`cascadeRmsGain` back out, so the trait becomes the _base_ scale and the realised
RMS is the target.

## 3. What is authored

Each asset declares a rhythm and two targets, and the two traits that cannot be
chosen independently of that rhythm are solved for:

| Declared               | Solved                                          |
| ---------------------- | ----------------------------------------------- |
| `cascadeDepth`         | `clustering` — depth is an exponent on the tail |
| `cascadeSpanMs`        | `volatility` — depth changes realised amplitude |
| `cascadeSpacing`       |                                                 |
| `regimeTempo`          |                                                 |
| `arrivalMemoryMs`      |                                                 |
| target excess kurtosis |                                                 |
| target tick RMS        |                                                 |

The targets are set to each asset's **existing** values. Tail weight and
amplitude are held where PH-4 validated them; rhythm does all of the
differentiating work. That is the cleanest available separation between "the
assets are more distinguishable" and "the assets were made more extreme".

## 4. The rhythms

| Asset  | Depth | Span | Spacing | Regime tempo | Arrival memory | Character                                                       |
| ------ | ----- | ---- | ------- | ------------ | -------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------- |
| eurusd | 13    | 36 h | 2.33    | 1.6          | 240 s          | Deep, wide ladder; holds a regime a long time. Institutional.   |
| gbpjpy | 7     | 8 h  | 3.6     | 0.55         | 40 s           | Few, widely separated rhythms; changes character often. Chunky. |
| btcusd | 16    | 30 h | 2.05    | 0.35         | 18 s           | Most timescales of any asset, restless regimes, sharp bursts.   |
| spx    | 8     | 44 h | 3.4     | 2.4          | 420 s          | Longest memory, slowest regime turnover, long gentle swells.    |
| xauusd | 11    | 4 h  | 2.15    | 0.9          | 110 s          | Memory that stops at four hours — no multi-session persistence. |

`xauusd` is the interesting one: a short span with a moderate depth means its
volatility autocorrelation is gone by the time the others' has barely started to
decay. That is a shape difference no amount of amplitude scaling can imitate.

## 5. Recalibration

Every recorded number in the catalogue was derived from the old process and is
now wrong. Each asset is recalibrated from its own 30-second return distribution
and re-records `logQuantum`, `displayPrecision`, `tieRate`, `medianSteps`,
`meanIntervalMs` and the achieved excess kurtosis.

Per PH-10.1 §5.1, the achieved kurtosis is computed against a **recorded stream
label**, and the achieved value is what gets recorded — not the requested one.

Historical figures stay in the phase records that produced them
(`GOVERNANCE.md` §47). PH-4's numbers described PH-4's process and remain true
of it.

## 6. Acceptance criteria

1. Every asset passes the analytic gate and lands inside the realism band.
2. Realised tick RMS is within 1% of each asset's pre-existing value — the
   constraint that makes the headline number mean something.
3. Every recorded calibration figure reproduces from fresh streams, to the bands
   `catalogue.stat.test.ts` already enforces.
4. The recorded traits reproduce from `authorPersonality` and the recorded
   targets, so the authoring is auditable and not just the calibration.
5. Shape-feature differentiation is measured against the permutation null and
   **reported as measured**, whatever it says.
6. Guardrails, lint, build and the unit suite pass.

## 7. Exclusions

Full revalidation — battery, withheld families, realism suite — is PH-10.3. This
subphase produces the catalogue; the next one decides whether it survives.

### What the subphase learned

**PH-4.3's ceiling did its job by being written down, and not by failing.** It
asserted shape differentiation stayed _below_ 45% specifically so that a genuine
improvement would break the test and force the claim to be rewritten. The new
figure came in at 40.5% — a real, significant improvement that slid under the
bound without tripping it. The mechanism worked anyway, because the _comment_
said what the assertion was for. A bound chosen to catch improvement has to be
set where improvement actually lands, and nobody can know that in advance; what
carried the intent across two phases was the sentence explaining it.

**A recorded measurement that nothing reads is a comment.**
`MEASURED_LATTICE_TIE_RATES` records the rate at which stakes are refunded under
ADR-0007. It was measured by Cycle Audit 2, exported, documented as evidence —
and read by no code and no test. Re-authoring the cascade moved every value in it
and nothing failed. It has a test now, and the test has teeth: a 40x quantum
must produce 16.93% ties against 0.5%.

**The binomial was wrong again, for the third time.** Writing that test surfaced
a second defect underneath the first. The recorded rates did not reproduce across
seeds — three of five moved three to four times the stated binomial error. The
reason is the one Cycle Audit 2 already found behind INV-007's p-value: 20,000
consecutive 30-second horizons are one realisation, not 20,000 independent draws,
because volatility clustering makes tie-proneness autocorrelated. Measured
between-replicate standard deviation is 0.10-0.19pp against a binomial 2se of
0.09pp — understated roughly fourfold.

Each rate is now a mean over 15 replicates across three unrelated stream
families. The limiting quantity is stated in the source: not horizons sampled but
independent volatility epochs simulated. One replicate spans 67 simulated hours
and the slowest cascade component turns over in 36 to 44 of them, so sampling
more horizons inside one run buys almost nothing.

That is the same fact as B-002, met from a third direction. It is worth naming as
a general property of this engine rather than a series of coincidences: **any
statistic of this market is limited by simulated duration, not by sample count,
because the volatility process has memory measured in days.**

### Known limitations carried forward

- Realised mean tick interval moved by up to 5.2% (spx, 3187 to 3352 ms) even
  though `tempoMs` is unchanged, because excitation memory interacts with the
  Hawkes intensity ceiling. Pace is divided out of the shape signature, so it
  cannot flatter the headline — but "only rhythm changed" is true of the traits,
  not of realised pace. Living code comments quoting the old range were corrected.
- Shape differentiation at 40.5% is real and significant but far from the
  near-perfect full-signature figure. Assets remain much easier to tell apart by
  size than by character.
- The lattice tie test costs 44 seconds of the statistical suite.
