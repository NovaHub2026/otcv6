# PH-39.1 — A Starved Reopening Is Re-Armed, And The Outage Keeps One Seam

## 1. Objective

Close the defect PH-39 §2 names: a market reopened past its catch-up bound that
starves again **before publishing its first tick** stays refused for the life of
the process. Close it without trading a dead market for a record full of
discontinuities nobody observed.

## 2. Three candidates, judged rather than picked

The first thing that turns the reproduction green is to delete the bound, and
that is not evidence that deleting it is right. Three candidates were analysed
independently — each one read the code, listed what it would change, what it
would break and what it would cost — and then put to two judges with different
lenses: one reading the invariants and the records, one reading the operator and
the broker.

| Option            | What it does                                                    |
| ----------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **A — re-arm**    | An unproductive reopening is armed again, without a second seam |
| B — interval only | Delete the bound; let `MIN_REOPEN_INTERVAL_MS` carry it alone   |
| C — backoff       | `MIN_REOPEN_INTERVAL_MS · 2ⁿ`, capped, reset on a publication   |

**A is built, with three corrections both judges asked for**: the stall stays set
on _every_ reopening, the property the silent re-arming rests on is **checked**
against the feed's own declaration rather than reasoned about, and the re-arming
count goes in the sentence an operator reads. What decided the mechanism, in the
analysis rather than in the preference:

- **B and C both recover by repeating a ceremony for an outage that never
  ended.** A reopening evicts every subscriber of that market (`feed.forget`),
  seals and restarts the commitment chain, writes a `RecoveryOutcome`, counts an
  outage and logs an unduplicated `REOPENED` line. Under a sustained starvation B
  does all of that sixty times an hour per asset — on thirty assets, ~1,800
  reopenings an hour in `otc_market_reopenings_total` and on the order of two
  hundred thousand log lines — for one outage.
- **The record cannot tell the difference, which is why the bound looked
  harmless.** A `RecordedSeam` is derived from two adjacent recorded ticks, so a
  reopening that publishes nothing writes nothing: B, C and A all leave exactly
  one recorded seam per outage. The cost of B and C is entirely in the surfaces
  around the record, and that is where this subphase's guards had to go.
- **C pays for its own rate limit with a dead market.** Any cap is a stretch of
  time after the load has passed in which the venue is still refusing to try.
- **A's recovery is one pass.** The moment the process gets its CPU back, the
  re-armed market is inside its bound and publishes.

## 3. What makes the re-arming safe, exactly

`HostedMarket.lastPublishedState` reports the tick **carried** across a resume or
a reopening, not only what this process emitted. A reopened market that has
published nothing therefore still carries the pre-outage tick, and
`reopenStalledMarket` derives everything it reserves from it:

- the price it opens at — the same one;
- the sequence it resumes at — `carried.sequence + DEFAULT_SEQUENCE_LEASE`, the
  same number;
- the key epoch — `startKeyEpoch(instant, previous)`, strictly forward, so every
  re-arming opens a keystream nothing has been drawn from (ADR-0019). The tick
  the previous arming drew was never served to anybody.

So a re-arming is indistinguishable from the first arming everywhere outside the
market: the feed's declared window, the sealed chain, the recovery outcome and
the gap the record will write are the ones already in place. It follows that the
re-arming may leave all four alone — and that is the whole difference between A
and B.

`packages/runtime/src/reopen.test.ts` holds that property directly: reopened
twice with nothing published in between, the second reopening reserves the same
sequence, carries the same price, and sits on a later key epoch.

## 4. What is built

**`VenueService.#reopen` splits the two cases.**

- A market that **has published** since its reopening and falls behind again has
  had a new outage: the whole seam, rate-limited by `MIN_REOPEN_INTERVAL_MS`,
  exactly as ADR-0020 says.
- A market **still awaiting its first tick** is re-armed: `reopenStalledMarket`,
  `venue.reopen`, `prime()`, the recovery outcome refreshed — and nothing else.
  No `feed.forget`, no `feed.seam`, no `seamChain`, no `reopenings`, and the
  stall is left for the caller to record.

**It returns `false` when it re-arms**, although it did act. That is how the
market goes on reporting itself stalled: `#pass` records the stall, `/health`
answers `degraded`, `/health/ready` refuses, `otc_markets_stalled` counts it and
the `STALLED` line is logged once per kind. A recovery that reported itself as a
running market would be Cycle Audit 6's finding (CA6-33) with the sign flipped.

**"Awaiting its first tick" is read off the market, not off the set.** The
re-arming is silent only while the market still carries the tick the first
reopening carried — `declared.fromSequence === market.lastPublishedSequence`. A
market whose ticks the **record refused** is also awaiting its first recorded
tick, but it carries a later one, so its reserved sequence has moved and the
declarations have to move with it: that case takes the whole seam, interval and
all.

**No reopening reports the market as running until it publishes.** The seam
branch used to end in `stalled.delete(assetId)`, which called a market healthy on
the strength of having been reopened — and a reopened market has served nothing
yet. One pass is short, but it is the pass in which a **checkpoint** is written:
`checkpoint()` skips a market only while it is stalled, and a clock jump past the
catch-up bound is also a clock jump past the checkpoint cadence, so every outage
refreshed `savedAt` for a market whose last published tick was hours old. Cycle
Audit 10 closed exactly that for a stalled market, and the reopening path was
never brought under it. The stall now clears where it always did — on the record
accepting the first tick — and `/health` reads `degraded` for one inter-arrival
after a reopening, which is true.

**The re-arming stays silent only against a declaration that still stands.** It is
read from `TickFeed.declaredSeam`, not inferred: the feed's `publishedThrough` is
the tick the reopening carried, and `publish` clears the declaration the moment a
window exists. A market whose ticks the **record refused** is also awaiting its
first recorded tick but carries a later one, so its reserved sequence has moved
and the declarations have to move with it — that case takes the whole seam,
interval and all.

**`otc_market_rearms_total`, and the count in the reason.** The seam counter is supposed to stay frozen while
a market is being re-armed, and during the live incident a frozen
`otc_market_reopenings_total` at 60 was exactly what made the state
unreadable — the mechanism had fired and stopped, and nothing said which. The
new counter rises while the venue is working the problem, and the venue is
stalled the whole time it does, so the pair says which of the two states a
deployment is in. The count also goes into the stall reason itself — `Reopened and
re-armed 4x at the clock, still awaiting its first tick` — because the reason
string in `/health` is what the operator of 2026-09-25 actually read, and it named
the lag and nothing else. A `RE-ARMED` line is logged once per run, not once per
pass (a6-05).

**And the venue says how late its own loop is.** `otc_seconds_since_last_pass` is
new. The catch-up bound is defined on that quantity and nothing exported it, so a
venue running a pass every 250 ms and one running a pass every fourteen seconds
looked identical — and the second is one pass away from stalling every market it
hosts. It is the warning the incident did not have. It is omitted before the
first completed pass rather than reported as zero, because zero would be a
claim.

## 5. What it costs, measured

Re-arming the whole catalogue costs **21.2 ms — 0.705 ms per asset** on this host
(thirty assets, `reopenStalledMarket` plus `prime()`, measured 2026-09-25 while
other work ran, so an upper bound for a loaded machine). At most one re-arming
per market per pass, and a pass only reaches this path when it is already more
than fifteen seconds late, so the work is bounded by the starvation that caused
it. The scheduler's own `STALLED_BACKOFF_MS` of 250 ms keeps a venue with every
market stalled from spinning while it does this.

## 6. Verified on the venue that kept dying

The fix was deployed to the live venue **before** its own phase gate, on purpose:
the gate is seventy-nine minutes of builds, three thousand unit tests and a
browser on the same host, which is the load that had already wedged this venue
three times that day. So the gate was the reproduction, and the venue was
sampled every twenty seconds while it ran.

| Over the 79 minutes of the gate, 218 samples |                                                  |
| -------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------ |
| load average                                 | mean 3.07, max 9.97                              |
| samples reporting `degraded`                 | **12 of 218**, every one recovered by the next   |
| markets stalled in the worst sample          | 30 of 30                                         |
| automatic reopenings                         | **600** — about one per asset every four minutes |
| re-armings                                   | 0 in this window                                 |
| ticks published                              | 510,897                                          |
| `otc_seconds_since_last_pass`                | median 0.010 s, p90 8.1 s, **max 107.5 s**       |
| operator interventions                       | **none**                                         |

The last two rows are the phase in one line. A host that stops giving this
process the CPU for a hundred and seven seconds — seven times the catch-up bound
— is exactly the condition that used to end with thirty dead markets and a
person restarting the service; the venue came back from it by itself, twelve
times. And the number that says the host did that is the gauge this subphase
added: nothing exported it before, so all three incidents of 2026-09-25 were
diagnosed from their consequences.

**What it costs is visible in the same table: six hundred recorded seams in an
hour and a quarter.** A venue that shares a host with a build farm takes an
outage every few minutes per asset, and each one is a real discontinuity a
broker must refuse to settle across. That is ADR-0020's trade, unchanged by this
subphase — it is not the price of the fix, it is the price of the host. A
deployment that sees this in production has a host problem, and now it has two
numbers that say so instead of a wedge.

**The re-arming path itself was exercised earlier the same afternoon**, on the
first build of this subphase under the unit suite: 239 reopenings, one
re-arming, thirty markets serving throughout. That single re-arming is the exact
pass that used to kill the venue for good.

**And the boot of that venue confirmed a finding the panel called plausible.**
At 12:31:40Z all thirty markets `resumed with a SEAM — the checkpoint is 20s old,
past the 15s catch-up bound`; at 12:32:06Z all thirty logged `REOPENED — Market
is 16s behind the clock`. Two recorded discontinuities per asset for one clean
restart, twenty-six seconds apart, because `start()` itself takes longer than the
bound it is measured against. That is
[#23](https://github.com/NovaHub2026/otcv6/issues/23), observed rather than
predicted, and it is not fixed here — the re-arming this subphase adds is the
operation a fix would use.

## 7. Guards, each watched failing

The reproduction first: three tests in `apps/api/src/venueReopen.test.ts` and one
in `packages/runtime/src/reopen.test.ts`, all red before the change.

| Plant                                                              | What went red                                                                |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| the original bound restored (`awaitingFirstTick` refuses for ever) | comes back by itself; records one seam for one outage                        |
| every starved pass takes the whole seam ceremony                   | 4 tests, including ADR-0020's own "does not reopen twice without publishing" |
| a re-arming returns `true`, so the stall is skipped                | 3 tests, led by "is not reported healthy while it is publishing nothing"     |
| a moved reserved sequence is re-armed silently                     | exactly 1, the moved-window guard                                            |
| the lease is reserved past every tick **drawn** rather than served | 2 of 8 in `reopen.test.ts`, led by the same-sequence guard                   |
| a reopening clears the stall again                                 | the checkpoint guard, and ADR-0020's "reopens again once both bounds"        |
| the pass-latency gauge measures the read instead of the pass       | exactly 1, the pass-latency guard                                            |
| the stall reason drops the re-arming count                         | exactly 1, the operator-reason guard                                         |

The second plant is the one that matters: it is the shortcut this subphase
exists to avoid, and the property that catches it is a count of ceremonies, not
a count of seams in the record — which is exactly what the analysis predicted no
record-level assertion could see.

## 8. What the panel found next door, and what was done with it

The agent that read the whole stall and reopen path for defects nobody had
written down returned seven, and refuted six of its own suspicions on the way —
among them that two `seamChain` calls could lose the chain binding, that a
reopening could leave a settleable undeclared gap, and that a candle could be
folded across the outage. All three were checked in the code and closed.

| Finding                                                                                                                          | Status                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| every reopening refreshed the checkpoint of a market that had published nothing, and the next boot resumes from it               | **fixed here** — the same change the judges asked for, with a guard of its own                                                                                                                                |
| `retire()` never cleared `reopenedAt` or `awaitingFirstTick`, which CA7-15's sweep exists to prevent                             | **fixed here**, and unguarded on purpose: nothing in this process re-hosts a retired id, so there is no failure to watch. Hygiene on a list whose own rule is "everything goes with it"                       |
| `awaitingFirstTick` is cleared before the `ticks.length === 0` check, so a batch the record deduped to nothing clears it         | **neutralised rather than patched** — the flag no longer decides anything by itself: a market that produced ticks has moved its carried sequence, so the declaration check refuses the silent path either way |
| the catch-up bound is checked twice, at two instants, with the whole of `start()` in between — a slow boot can seam every market | **carried**. It is the resume path, not this one, and it is plausible here: thirty assets against a 250,000-tick record                                                                                       |
| a reopening writes into the publication directory before the writer-lock renewal that would stop it                              | **carried**. Multi-node only; this deployment sets no `OTC_PUBLICATION_DIR`                                                                                                                                   |
| `HostedMarket.exhausted` stops a market with no stall, no failure and no metric — CA6-33's shape on the one path with no surface | **carried**. Unreachable in production, where no engine is given a tick limit                                                                                                                                 |

The three carried findings are filed rather than left in a phase document:
[#23](https://github.com/NovaHub2026/otcv6/issues/23) (the boot that outruns its
own bound, **observed live** — see §6),
[#24](https://github.com/NovaHub2026/otcv6/issues/24) and
[#25](https://github.com/NovaHub2026/otcv6/issues/25).

## 9. What the first gate measured, and what it did not

**A working venue on this host is load, and the gate measures it.** The first
PH-39 gate went red on three statistical tests — two browser assertions that
read an empty chart and one stream resume refused — while the venue this session
had just repaired was publishing thirty markets on the same box. The rpc probe
recorded a 15.9 s block inside the browser worker and the venue's own new gauge
recorded the host withholding the CPU for up to 107 s. The same two files, alone,
on a quiet host: 12 tests, 12 passed. Then the full gate, with the venue stopped:
green.

Nothing was wrong with the tests and nothing was wrong with the change. What is
worth carrying is the asymmetry: **every gate measured on this machine before
2026-09-25 ran beside a venue that was wedged**, and a wedged venue consumes
nothing. A venue that works does. Stop the engine before a gate — or accept that
the numbers in this file describe a different machine.

## 10. What this does not do

- **It does not change what a reopening is.** The seam, its price, its sequence,
  its key epoch and its refusals are ADR-0020's, unmoved. What changed is when a
  second one may be taken, and what a venue says about itself while it is not
  serving.
- **It does not move either of ADR-0020's bounds for a market that published.**
  A market that served something and then fell behind is a new outage, and it
  waits the minute.
- **It does not make the venue survive a host that never gives the CPU back.**
  Nothing can. What it does is stop the venue from needing an operator once the
  host recovers.
