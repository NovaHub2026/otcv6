## 1. Objective

A leader dies and another takes over: no duplicate keystream consumption, no
forked record, and a seam that is visible rather than papered over.

## 2. The failover a seam makes, and why the log refused it

`resumeMarket` has two branches (PH-5.2). The one that continues is safe by
construction and needs nothing new here: the restored engine regenerates the
ticks the dead leader published but never checkpointed, deterministic replay
makes them identical, and PH-14.2's idempotent append accepts them. That is the
mechanism working exactly as designed, and it is the common case.

The other branch is the problem. A seam moves the sequence **past the reserved
block** — a jump of `DEFAULT_SEQUENCE_LEASE`, deliberately, because "the gap is
visible and free; a duplicate is neither" (PH-5.2). And PH-14.2's replication log
refuses a gap:

> a gap in the record would be served to every observer as though it were the
> market

Both rules are right and they contradict. The resolution is not to relax either:
it is that **a discontinuity has to be recorded as one**. A seam is a fact about
the record, so the record carries it — a `SeamMarker` entry sitting between the
last sequence before and the first after, fenced like every other write.

That turns the contradiction into the feature the phase title asks for. The gap
cannot be silent, because the only way to write past it is to say so.

## 3. The record is entries, not ticks

Once a seam is a thing the record holds, `readTicks` is the wrong shape: it
returns ticks and a client reading across a seam would see two runs of ticks with
no indication that anything separates them.

So the log yields **entries** — a tick or a seam — and a client asking for a
sequence that fell inside a gap gets the seam first, then the ticks. Nobody can
read across a discontinuity without being told about it, which is exactly the
property that was missing.

## 4. A price is not carried across a seam

`priceAt` answers with the last tick at or before an instant. Between the last
tick before a seam and the first after it, that would report a price for a window
in which nothing was published and no node was generating.

It reports **null** instead, and `spansSeam(from, to)` says whether a window
crosses one. The reason is ADR-0010's, unchanged: a contract whose entry and
expiry sit either side of an unobserved interval cannot be settled honestly
against it, and the settlement path needs to be able to ask rather than to
assume continuity.

This is deliberately narrow. An instant _after_ the newest tick still reports the
newest price — an idle market between ticks is not a discontinuity. Only a
recorded seam suppresses the answer.

## 5. The leader loop

PH-14.2 drove the leader by hand, which was right for testing INV-002 and wrong
as a deliverable. `LeaderSession` is the loop:

1. renew the lease — and if it is lost, **stop**, before generating anything;
2. advance the market;
3. record a pending seam, once, at the first tick that follows it, because that
   is the moment its resuming sequence and instant are known;
4. append under the fence;
5. checkpoint on a cadence.

Order matters at step 1. A session that advanced first and renewed afterwards
would generate ticks it has no authority to publish, and the fence would refuse
them _after_ the keystream had already been spent.

## 6. What failover cannot repair, and does not pretend to

Between a leader's death and its successor's first tick, no node was generating.
The lease term bounds it at 15 seconds plus the successor's polling interval.

If the successor resumes and catches up within `DEFAULT_MAX_CATCH_UP_MS`, the
record is continuous and ADR-0010's criterion holds: no contract the product
sells can span the burst. Beyond that the catch-up is refused, the market seams,
and the record shows the hole.

There is no third option in which the missing interval comes back. Inventing it
would mean publishing prices nobody observed — the thing ADR-0010 exists to
refuse and the thing ADR-0012 shows a second generating node cannot avoid.

### Found while building it

**The seam must continue the record, not the checkpoint.** The first
`LeaderSession` took the seam's `lastSequence` from `RecoveryOutcome`, which
carries the _checkpoint's_ last published sequence. A dead leader appends every
tick it publishes and checkpoints only on a cadence, so the record routinely runs
ahead of the state its successor resumes from — by exactly the ticks that were
published and never persisted. The seam would then have claimed a sequence the
record had already passed, and `recordSeam` would have refused it, correctly.
It reads the record head instead. The eleventh plant above is that mistake,
re-made deliberately.

### Fixed in scope

`readTicks` became `readRecord` and returns entries. Ticks alone were the wrong
shape once the record could hold a discontinuity: a client reading across one
would have seen two runs of ticks with nothing between them, and nothing in the
protocol would have told it not to interpolate.
