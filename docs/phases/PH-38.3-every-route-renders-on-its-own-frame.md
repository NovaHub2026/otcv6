# PH-38.3 — Every Read Route Renders On The Frame The Price Was Written In

Type: SUBPHASE TECHNICAL DOCUMENT
Identifier: PH-38.3
Parent: [PH-38](PH-38-the-frame-a-price-counts-in.md)
Status: ACTIVE
Created: 2026-09-24

---

## 1. Objective

Make the venue answer with the price it published. PH-38.1 gave the record
somewhere to say what its integers count in and PH-38.2 let an operator declare
what the old ones counted in; neither changed a single answer a broker receives.
This is the subphase where the defect stops.

## 2. What was wrong

`published()` paired every recorded tick with `asset.instrument` — the live
catalogue entry, the frame in force **now**. So the moment PH-37 moved all
thirty lattices, every read route began rendering the whole retained past on a
lattice it was never written on: 3,728,119 of 7,500,278 retained ticks, 30 of
30 assets, a median error of 31.8% and a worst case of 1,483%.

## 3. What is built

**`published()` takes the frame.** It is resolved per tick from the record's own
frame log by sequence, not from the catalogue. A deployment that keeps no record
still renders on the live instrument, because there the only ticks a route can
reach are ones this process published under the frame it is running now.

**An undeclared range answers `null`, not today's frame.** `displayPrice`,
`logQuantum` and `referencePrice` are all null when the venue cannot say what
the integer counted in. The integer itself is never null: it is the settlement
primitive and it is not in doubt. A null is a statement; a wrong number is not.

**Every published tick carries its frame.** `logQuantum` and `referencePrice`
travel with the integer, so a broker that archived integers renders them itself
instead of asking once per tick.

**`GET /markets/:id/lattices`** lists every frame the record has held, oldest
first, half-open by sequence — the join table for an archive, one request
instead of one per tick. The exact sibling of `/markets/:id/seams`.

**Contract 3.0.0**, and major rather than minor on purpose. The route is
additive but `displayPrice: 'string|null'` is not: a broker whose parser rejects
null will fail on exactly the deep-history requests it makes to reconcile a
statement. That is the change being announced, and announcing it is the point of
a version.

## 4. The guard a broker runs

A check in the conformance suite, `a recorded price states the frame it counts
in`: the broker re-derives the venue's own `displayPrice` from the response's
own `logQuantum` and `referencePrice`. Agreement means the two numbers travel
with the integer; disagreement means they do not, whatever the contract claims.
A venue that answers `displayPrice: null` passes — it is holding the integer and
saying it cannot date it, which is the honest answer.

It is watched failing two ways. A new fault, `latticeMismatch`, serves a tick
whose stated frame does not produce its own `displayPrice` — the exact shape a
venue has when it renders a recorded price on the lattice in force now — and the
suite names the check. And with the check's predicate replaced by `true`, that
fault test goes red, so the check is load-bearing rather than decorative.

## 5. What is deliberately left

The candle history is not converted here; a bar folded across a frame boundary
is PH-38.4's subject. `describe()` is unchanged because it renders the live
tick, which is on today's frame by definition. The multi-node replication log
still carries prices with no frame, and that is named rather than fixed.
