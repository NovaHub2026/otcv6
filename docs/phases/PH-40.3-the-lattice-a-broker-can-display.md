## 1. Objective

A move the engine made must never print as no move. That is a fairness property
at the one place the engine meets money: a broker settles on a price it shows,
and the broker this phase serves compares **display strings**. Make it true at
the engine's own published precision on every asset, across the range a price
actually visits; measure what it costs a broker that shows fewer decimals than
the engine publishes; and put the one decision that is not the engine's in front
of the Human Owner with the numbers.

## 2. Two findings, measured

**At the engine's own precision (an engine defect).** A step is
`price × logQuantum`, so it shrinks as the price falls, and the rule that chose
each asset's decimals guaranteed one displayed digit only _at the reference
price_. Six assets were inside a 50% fall of hiding a step:

| asset       | digits per step at reference | a step hides below |
| ----------- | ---------------------------- | ------------------ |
| meta-otc    | 1.03                         | 557.8 (−3%)        |
| tsla-otc    | 1.35                         | 251.5 (−26%)       |
| bnbusdt-otc | 1.61                         | 404.9 (−38%)       |
| tcx-idx-otc | 1.62                         | 615.6 (−38%)       |
| btcusdt-otc | 1.68                         | 41,650 (−40%)      |
| aix-idx-otc | 1.94                         | 516.1 (−48%)       |

**It happened, not only could.** The live record shows `meta-otc` trading from
553.25 to 566.38 on its current frame: below 557.8, adjacent prices rendered
the same two decimals, so the `displayPrice` the engine published called a real
move no move while `settle()` — which compares integers — did not.

**At a broker's five decimals (the Owner's decision).** Orbit caps
TradingView's `pricescale` at 100,000 and formats every trading screen to five
decimals (two above 10,000), and settles by comparing those strings. Six assets
step finer than one fifth decimal. Measured over 4 × 2,000 windows per horizon:

| asset     | step / 5th decimal | engine refunds @30 s | at 5 decimals @30 s | @60 s  | @5 min | @15 min |
| --------- | ------------------ | -------------------- | ------------------- | ------ | ------ | ------- |
| USD/CHF   | 0.24               | 3.80%                | **15.83%**          | 11.20% | 4.81%  | 2.65%   |
| EUR/GBP   | 0.25               | 3.72%                | **14.69%**          | 10.08% | 4.40%  | 2.38%   |
| AUD/USD   | 0.41               | 4.47%                | **11.31%**          | 8.00%  | 3.36%  | 1.93%   |
| GBP/USD   | 0.55               | 4.31%                | **8.16%**           | 5.66%  | 2.20%  | 1.26%   |
| EUR/USD   | 0.47               | 3.57%                | **7.71%**           | 5.42%  | 2.38%  | 1.27%   |
| DOGE/USDT | 0.58               | 4.04%                | **7.58%**           | 5.41%  | 2.32%  | 1.21%   |

Controls, identical in both columns: XRP 4.19%, USD/JPY 4.22%, TSLA 4.44% at
30 s. On those six, a broker showing five decimals refunds two to four times
what the engine settles as a tie, above the Human Owner's 5% ceiling at 30 s
and 60 s — and it disagrees with `settle()` on every one of the extra ties.

## 3. What is built (the engine's half)

- **`DISPLAY_MARGIN = 2`** (`packages/engine/src/asset.ts`): an asset's decimals
  are chosen so one step is at least one displayed digit down to half its
  reference price. Six catalogue precisions change: meta, tsla, bnb, tcx, aix
  2 → 3; btc 0 → 1. No quantum and no reference changes, so no integer moves, no
  refund rate moves, and nothing re-expresses.
- **A change of display precision alone is not a change of unit.** It writes a
  new frame epoch (the precision is part of the frame) but settlement, the
  conformance suite and the chart treat it as the same unit: `reframedSequences`
  already ignored it; `frameOfSpan` and the history route now date a bar across
  it at the finer precision instead of blanking every bar across the release;
  and the conformance rule "every frame boundary is a seam" (Cycle Audit 13,
  a5-01) applies to changes of unit, so a release that only makes a display
  finer may land on a quick restart. The checkpoint fingerprint never included
  the precision, so such a release resumes without a seam.

## 4. The decision that is not the engine's

For the six at five decimals there are three honest answers, and the engine
already supports the first:

1. **The broker shows the engine's precision** (`pricescale = 10^displayPrecision`,
   six decimals on those six): refunds stay at the engine's 3.6%–4.5%, and the
   broker's string comparison agrees with `settle()` on every contract. It is a
   change in the broker's frontend, which this phase does not make.
2. **The engine coarsens those six lattices** to at least one fifth decimal:
   refunds 6.8%–13.2% at 30 s — above the ceiling, a little below what five
   decimals refund today — and every broker agrees with every other.
3. **Raise the ceiling for those six.**

Recommended: (1). It keeps the Owner's ceiling, costs the engine nothing, and the
other 24 assets already work at five decimals.

## 5. Guards, each watched failing

| Plant                                             | What went red                                                                                        |
| ------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `meta-otc` back to two decimals                   | `meta-otc displays at least as finely as it settles, down to half its reference price`               |
| `DISPLAY_MARGIN` back to 1                        | `chooses decimals that keep a step visible down to half the reference price` (the registration path) |
| `frameOfSpan` splits on any epoch                 | `treats a change of display precision alone as no change, anywhere`                                  |
| the history route refuses a bar across any epoch  | `dates a bar across a change of display precision alone, on the finer precision`                     |
| conformance rule 4 demands a seam for every epoch | `owes no seam for a change of display precision alone, and still one for a change of unit`           |
| the checkpoint fingerprint includes the precision | `a release that only makes the display finer writes a frame, owes no seam, …`                        |

**One plant survived first, and the reason is worth keeping.** Setting
`DISPLAY_MARGIN` back to 1 turned nothing red, because the catalogue test read the
margin from the constant it was testing: a weaker rule weakened the test with it
— the shape of the 2.58x-wrong value that once passed 3,529 tests. The
requirement is now a literal in the catalogue test, and the registration path,
which computes its precision rather than reading a recorded one, has an assertion
of its own. Both were then watched failing against the weaker rule.

**The upgrade path has a test with its real input.** The bar-dating tests
declare their frames by hand; the last test lets two releases write them — the
first under the old precision, a quick restart under the new — and asserts the
frame log, the absence of a seam, and that each tick renders at the precision it
was published at. Without it, a fingerprint change that forced a seam on every
such release passed the rest of the suite.

## 6. Verification

The targeted gate (GOVERNANCE §21) on `9b16be6`, `TARGETED_EXIT=0`: format, build, `typecheck:web`, `typecheck:config`, lint, the unit suite (179 files / 3,662 tests) and the ten statistical suites that render, register or serve a price — the panel in a real browser, the panel surface, registration, the asset lifecycle, the served record, both catalogue suites, conformance against a live venue, restart and operations (10 files / 59 tests, 1,353 s).

**Two runs went red before it, and one of them is not explained.** The first
failed in the unit suite on the broker-facing `CATALOGUE.md`, which is generated
from the catalogue and lists each asset's decimals: stale, regenerated
(`97cc2de`). The second failed twice in the browser panel suite — zero bars on
the chart after an asset switch, and after a switch to 5m — and was reproduced
once alone. Then the same suite passed on PH-40.2's commit, and on this commit
five times running. What was established: the engine served 288 dated 5m bars
for EUR/USD from a venue booted exactly as the test boots it; neither asset the
test ends on changed precision; and PH-40.3's two frame changes refuse strictly
fewer bars than before. What was not: the cause. The failures fell between 05:26
and 05:40 UTC and every run since has passed. Rather than leave the next one as
unreadable, the bar-count assertions now carry the chart's own bar count,
undated count and stream state in their message (`9b16be6`).
