## 1. Objective

Build [ADR-0021](../decisions/ADR-0021-a-contract-settles-at-its-final-millisecond.md)
as the Human Owner decided it on 2026-09-26: a contract settles at its final
millisecond, with the price in force at that millisecond, whatever happened to
the venue inside its window; and nothing the engine serves treats a seam as a
closed market. Keep one refusal, and make it impossible to reach by silence: a
window across a **change of lattice**.

## 2. What the engine did before

Since Cycle Audit 5, `settle()` refused any contract whose window touched a
recorded seam, and since PH-31 `GET /markets/:id/price?at=` answered `409` for an
instant inside one. A broker turns both into a refund. On the day PH-39's live
record measured, that was **20.5% of 15-minute contracts and 1.7% of 30-second
ones** among those opened while the market was publishing — a host problem sold
to the trader as a product event.

The refusal protected nothing ADR-0020 had not already protected. A seam reopens
the market at the price it last published, so the last tick before the gap is
the price every observer was shown at every instant inside it and the price the
market resumed from; the reopening draws from a fresh keystream (ADR-0019), so
P(up) = P(down) after it exactly as before; and the missed interval is still
never generated (ADR-0010).

**Never before the final millisecond** is not new code: `settle()` already refused
an expiry beyond the record, and `/price` already answered `400` for an instant
after the newest published tick. What is new is that it is the _only_ reason a
contract waits, and that it is asserted beside the seam rule (§6).

**A result fixed inside a seam is reported late, never changed.** A contract whose
final millisecond falls in a gap can only be settled once the record reaches past
that millisecond, because until then nobody can know no tick was published at it.
The outcome is the price in force at the final millisecond either way; the
notification arrives late by at most the length of the seam.

## 4. What still refuses, and why it is a guard

A release that recalibrates an asset changes its lattice, and it can only do that
through a resume — so its epoch starts at a seam. Across that seam the entry and
expiry integers count in different units: comparing them decides the contract by
rounding, and Cycle Audit 13 measured a 9,900 win on a price that had not moved.
`settle()` refuses there, and PH-40.3 — which changes lattices — has to say how a
deployment does it with no contracts in flight. The refusal exists to make that
operator error loud; it is not expected to fire.

## 5. The unsafe answer is a false "no", and the first version gave it

`reframesAt` as first written looked for an epoch starting **exactly** at the
seam's `resumesAtSequence`. A change of lattice logged one sequence off inside a
wider gap then read as an ordinary seam, and a contract would have settled across
two units as though they were one. The conformance suite does assert that every
epoch after the first starts at a seam's resume (Cycle Audit 13, a5-01), but a
settlement guard that is only as good as another check's coverage is not a guard.
It now looks for a change anywhere after the last tick before the gap and at or
before the first one after it, and the test that says so fails against the
resume-only version (§6).

## 7. What this does not do

- **The heartbeat.** A broker's own freshness rule — Orbit's frontend refuses a
  quote older than fifteen seconds — still closes a market the engine considers
  open while a seam lasts. That is PH-40.2.
- **The multi-node follower.** `FollowerMarket.priceAt` still returns `null`
  inside a seam. No route serves it and a single-node deployment never reads it;
  it is carried to close before multi-node settlement is offered
  (`MULTI_NODE_AND_OPERATIONS.md`).
- **A change of lattice that is not at a seam.** `settle()` only sees seams. A
  frame change mid-stream is the venue invariant the conformance suite checks
  (a5-01), not something this subphase makes settle-safe.
