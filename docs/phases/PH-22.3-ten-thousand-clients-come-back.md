# PH-22.3 — What Happens When Ten Thousand Clients Come Back At Once

Type: SUBPHASE TECHNICAL DOCUMENT
Identifier: PH-22.3
Parent: [PH-22](PH-22-distribution-under-thousands-of-observers.md)
Status: APPROVED
Created: 2026-09-03

---

## 1. Objective

Characterise the failure case, and put a measured number where a chosen one is.

Everything measured so far is steady state: connections that arrive gradually
and stay. The case that decides whether this survives production is the other
one — **a deploy, and every client comes back at the same moment**, each asking
to resume from where it was.

## 2. What is unknown, precisely

**The replay bound is a number somebody picked.** Cycle Audit 7 found the
endpoint buffering an entire 50,000-tick window for one client that could not
read — 3.46 MiB accumulated in the handler's heap, the subscription neither
cancelled nor ended (CA7-04). The fix bounded it at **1 MB**, and that megabyte
was reasoned about, not measured. Ten thousand clients reconnecting is exactly
the case it exists for and it has never been run.

**Resumption is the expensive part of a reconnect, and it is unmeasured.** A
steady-state delivery is one tick; a resume is up to a megabyte of replay,
served before the connection does anything else. The two are different
experiments and only the first has been done.

**The panel refetches history on reconnect.** Each client asks for candles as
well as ticks, so a storm is a query storm against SQLite at the same moment it
is a replay storm against the feed.

## 3. What this subphase measures

1. **Simultaneous arrival.** N clients connecting inside one second, against N
   arriving over a minute, at the same final count. Same steady state, different
   transient.
2. **Resumption depth.** Clients resuming at the live edge, against clients
   resuming far enough back to trigger real replay. What the megabyte costs when
   everybody wants it at once.
3. **What breaks first**, and whether it says so: heap, event-loop lag, refused
   connections, or the replay bound doing its job and disconnecting people.
4. **Whether anyone is silently wrong afterwards.** Gaps and duplicates per
   asset, through the storm. A reconnect that skips is the defect the whole
   resume contract exists to prevent, and a storm is where it would first show.

## 4. What this subphase may not do

**It may not tune the bound to make the number look better.** If 1 MB is wrong,
the measurement says what it should be and why; a bound chosen to pass its own
test is not a bound.

**It may not treat a disconnection as a failure.** Disconnecting a client that
cannot keep up is the contract — delivering everything in order or stopping,
never a silent summary of what it missed. A storm where slow clients are
dropped, told, and reconnect is a working storm.

## 5. Acceptance criteria

1. A simultaneous arrival of at least 2,000 clients is measured against a gradual
   one, and the transient is characterised: peak heap, peak event-loop lag,
   refusals, and time to steady state.
2. The replay bound is measured under simultaneous resumption, and either
   confirmed at 1 MB with the number that justifies it, or changed with the
   number that justifies the change.
3. Zero gaps and zero duplicates per asset across the storm, or a finding.
4. Whatever breaks first is named, with the measurement that names it.

## 6. Result

Recorded in [`CYCLE-8-OBSERVER-LOAD.md`](../evidence/CYCLE-8-OBSERVER-LOAD.md).

**A storm on its own is nothing.** Two thousand clients connecting in the same
instant all connect inside 190 ms, with the engine at a fifth of what a gradual
arrival cost.

**A storm that resumes is the problem.** The same two thousand, resuming five
thousand sequences back, took **1,470 MB** of resident memory and **5.1 s** to
connect. Nobody was dropped and nobody was silently wrong — the contract held —
but ten thousand clients on that shape is roughly six gigabytes, past the heap
and past the machine.

CA7-04's per-connection bound of 1 MB was working exactly as designed and did
nothing, because **the quantity that matters in a storm is the sum** and nothing
was counting it.

## 7. What the fix got wrong first

The budget's first version counted bytes buffered **before** the response
headers, and the test written for it is what said so: the handler is synchronous,
so only one connection is ever in that state and the counter was always zero when
the next one looked. The guard could not be made to fail against a plant that
removed the ceiling, and that is what exposed it.

The 1.47 GB was never in that buffer. It was in the write buffers of two
thousand sockets that could not drain as fast as a replay filled them — which
Node exposes as `writableLength`, and which is what the budget counts now.

**A second defect, from the same change, would not boot the service at all.** The
ceiling was made a constructor parameter so it could be tested with a small
number, and a bare `number` parameter makes Nest read `Number` from
`design:paramtypes` and try to resolve it as a provider. Every unit test passed;
the service refused to start, and it surfaced in a load run. The identical trap
is documented two parameters above the one that caused it.

`module.test.ts` now guards it — and **its own first version was vacuous**,
reading `design:paramtypes` at runtime when the unit project transforms with
esbuild, which does not emit decorator metadata. It read an empty array and
passed on every input including the defect it was written for. It reads the
source text now, like every other guardrail here, and it asserts it found
constructors before it checks them.

## 8. Acceptance

1. **Met.** Simultaneous against gradual arrival at 1,500–2,000, characterised:
   connect percentiles, engine CPU, peak RSS, refusals.
2. **Partly met, and said so.** The bound is measured under simultaneous
   resumption — 1,470 MB, which is what justified replacing it — and the
   replacement is guarded by unit tests, each watched failing. But **the ceiling
   did not engage in the post-fix run**, because the harness reads as fast as
   the server writes and no connection ever owes anything. The improvement from
   1,470 MB to 458 MB is not attributable to it.
3. **Met.** Zero gaps and zero duplicates per asset across every storm.
4. **Met.** What breaks first is memory, in socket write buffers, under
   simultaneous resumption — named with the measurement that names it.

## 9. Approval

**APPROVED** 2026-09-03, with criterion 2 carried as a known limit rather than
claimed: the ceiling under a genuinely slow fleet is unmeasured, and closing
that needs a harness that reads slowly on purpose. Recorded as the next honest
step in the evidence rather than as a finished claim.
