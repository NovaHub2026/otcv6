# PH-22.3 — What Happens When Ten Thousand Clients Come Back At Once

Type: SUBPHASE TECHNICAL DOCUMENT
Identifier: PH-22.3
Parent: [PH-22](PH-22-distribution-under-thousands-of-observers.md)
Status: ACTIVE
Created: 2026-09-03

---

## 1. Objective

Characterise the failure case, and put a measured number where a chosen one is.

Everything measured so far is steady state: connections that arrive gradually
and stay. The case that decides whether this survives production is the other
one — **a deploy, and every client comes back at the same moment**, each asking
to resume from where it was.

## 2. What is unknown, precisely

**The replay bound is a number somebody picked.** Cycle Audit 7 found the
endpoint buffering an entire 50,000-tick window for one client that could not
read — 3.46 MiB accumulated in the handler's heap, the subscription neither
cancelled nor ended (CA7-04). The fix bounded it at **1 MB**, and that megabyte
was reasoned about, not measured. Ten thousand clients reconnecting is exactly
the case it exists for and it has never been run.

**Resumption is the expensive part of a reconnect, and it is unmeasured.** A
steady-state delivery is one tick; a resume is up to a megabyte of replay,
served before the connection does anything else. The two are different
experiments and only the first has been done.

**The panel refetches history on reconnect.** Each client asks for candles as
well as ticks, so a storm is a query storm against SQLite at the same moment it
is a replay storm against the feed.

## 3. What this subphase measures

1. **Simultaneous arrival.** N clients connecting inside one second, against N
   arriving over a minute, at the same final count. Same steady state, different
   transient.
2. **Resumption depth.** Clients resuming at the live edge, against clients
   resuming far enough back to trigger real replay. What the megabyte costs when
   everybody wants it at once.
3. **What breaks first**, and whether it says so: heap, event-loop lag, refused
   connections, or the replay bound doing its job and disconnecting people.
4. **Whether anyone is silently wrong afterwards.** Gaps and duplicates per
   asset, through the storm. A reconnect that skips is the defect the whole
   resume contract exists to prevent, and a storm is where it would first show.

## 4. What this subphase may not do

**It may not tune the bound to make the number look better.** If 1 MB is wrong,
the measurement says what it should be and why; a bound chosen to pass its own
test is not a bound.

**It may not treat a disconnection as a failure.** Disconnecting a client that
cannot keep up is the contract — delivering everything in order or stopping,
never a silent summary of what it missed. A storm where slow clients are
dropped, told, and reconnect is a working storm.

## 5. Acceptance criteria

1. A simultaneous arrival of at least 2,000 clients is measured against a gradual
   one, and the transient is characterised: peak heap, peak event-loop lag,
   refusals, and time to steady state.
2. The replay bound is measured under simultaneous resumption, and either
   confirmed at 1 MB with the number that justifies it, or changed with the
   number that justifies the change.
3. Zero gaps and zero duplicates per asset across the storm, or a finding.
4. Whatever breaks first is named, with the measurement that names it.
