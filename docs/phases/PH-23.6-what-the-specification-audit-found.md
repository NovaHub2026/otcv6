## 1. Objective

Close the two defects
[LAB-SPECIFICATION-AUDIT-001](../audits/LAB-SPECIFICATION-AUDIT-001.md) found
by execution rather than by reading: **LA-01**, a shock "intervention" the
signs cannot select, and **LA-02**, a candle close and a settlement price that
name different ticks when the engine prints on a boundary millisecond.

The other six findings are the distance between a mechanism and a product —
nothing is ever applied to a hosted market, no candle addressing, no presets,
no positions, seven scenarios of sixteen — and they are phases, not fixes.

## 2. LA-01 — a detector wearing the name of an intervention

`INTERVENTIONS.shock(size)` accepted any continuation containing a single-tick
displacement of at least `size`. A displacement is `sign × step` and its
absolute value is the step; the steps do not depend on the signs (ADR-0003,
verified on the shipped engine by `stepIndependence.test.ts`). So the predicate
was constant across every sign vector — executed: `shock(9)` accepted at attempt
1 under five seeds, `shock(10)` exhausted under five seeds, while `touches(30)`
took 2, 8, 1, 9, 1. Its docstring said _"keep one containing a displacement
above a threshold"_, describing a choice that was not being made.

**What changed.** A shock is a magnitude event, and the honest thing the Lab can
say about one is whether the engine is about to produce it and at which tick.
`nextShock(steps, size)` says that. What the signs _can_ decide is its
direction, so `INTERVENTIONS.directionAt(tick, ±1)` is the selection — accepted
about half the time, and the acceptance rate says so. "A positive shock" is the
composition: locate, then choose the sign.

**The guard.** Every entry of `INTERVENTIONS` is exercised at a parameter inside
its own range over 2,000 random sign vectors and must accept some and reject
others; the table of parameters is keyed by name and compared against the
object's keys, so a new criterion has to be added there — with a discriminating
parameter — before it can exist.

## 3. LA-02 — two rules for "the price at the end of the candle"

The chart's bucket is `[start, end)`: a tick at exactly `end` opens the next
candle. Settlement reads `priceAtOrBefore(expiry)`, inclusive. A contract
expiring at a candle boundary therefore settles on the next candle's first tick
when there is one — reproduced with a folded candle closing at 110 against a
settlement of 120, and measured on the shipped engine at one 1m candle in 1,163
(EUR/USD) and 471 (BTC/USD). PH-23 §3 had called this "already INV-003 and
already true", and no test related the two.

**What changed.** [ADR-0017](../decisions/ADR-0017-the-expiry-price-is-inclusive.md):
neither rule moves. Settlement's is the rule the battery samples with and every
recorded outcome was computed with (INV-009), and it is what real venues do; the
chart's is what every charting platform does and what coarser-timeframe folding
depends on. The two are allowed to differ by exactly the boundary tick, that is
now a **tested** property, and Candle Close Control is defined against the
settlement price — the Lab's selection window already keeps a tick at the
window's end.

PH-23 §3 and §9 are corrected to say what is true.

## 5. Acceptance criteria

1. No entry of `INTERVENTIONS` is constant across sign vectors, asserted
   behaviourally over every entry.
2. A shock is located, not selected; its direction is selected, with a measured
   rate.
3. A candle's close and the expiry price at its end are the same tick when
   nothing prints on the boundary, and differ by exactly that tick when
   something does — asserted through `settle` and `foldTicks`, not reimplemented.
4. PH-23's claim about §39 is corrected in place.
5. A targeted gate passes: the full quality gate and the full unit suite. The
   statistical suite is not re-run: no statistical test covers `intervention.ts`
   or the settlement boundary, and the full suite passed on this tree at
   `5d4f8f9` two hours earlier (`GOVERNANCE.md` §21).
