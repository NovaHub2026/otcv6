## 1. The two questions

**Can a hundred assets be built from eight families without INV-007 becoming
false?** The id enters the key derivation (ADR-0002), so a hundred assets can
share a personality and share no prices — but INV-007 says assets have
_genuinely distinct statistical personalities_, and twenty copies of one
personality are statistically one asset with twenty names.

**Can an asset be stopped from running away without a directional rule?** The
Human Owner asked for limits on how far a price can travel. A price ceiling
makes `P(down) > P(up)` near the bound, which is INV-006 broken in the most
visible way this system could manage.

## 2. Families are regions, not templates

Eight archetypes, each a **box** in trait space, and every asset a fresh draw
from one. Nothing is copied. Two assets from one archetype differ in tempo, in
cascade depth, in how long their volatility remembers, and in how far they
diffuse, while still reading as the same kind of market.

`clustering` and `volatility` appear in no box. They are solved — the first from
the sampled tail-weight target, the second from the sampled dispersion budget —
because cascade depth is an exponent on tail weight and sampling them directly
is how PH-3 reached an excess kurtosis of 1366.

Three joint constraints a box of independent ranges cannot express:

- **The fastest cascade component must be slower than half a tick.** Span,
  spacing, depth and tempo decide it together, so the spacing range is narrowed
  at sample time to the feasible part rather than the corner being refused.
  `assertArchetypeFeasible` checks at module load that something is left to
  narrow to, at the worst corner: deepest cascade, shortest span, fastest tempo.
- **The safety gate runs before the solve**, on a `clustering` that is about to
  be replaced. `startingClustering` holds `(1 + c²)^depth` at 1.6 — where the
  five hand-authored assets sit, from 1.36 to 1.79 — so every draw arrives inside
  the realism band whatever its depth.
- **Times are sampled on a log axis.** The distance between 500 ms and 1 s is the
  distance between 1 s and 2 s.

## 3. The dispersion budget

Each family declares a band for σ of the terminal log return over a quarter,
from 1.0–2.2% for a blue-chip index to 48–68% for an alt-coin. Chosen at
authoring time, blind to every price the asset will ever print.

**The measurement was already there.** The calibration accumulates a
full-precision walk and windows it, so the mean square of those windowed returns
over the horizon is the diffusion rate — `logVariancePerMs`. Signs are
independent fair coins, so returns are uncorrelated at every lag and variance is
additive in time: a ten-day run states a quarter's spread without simulating one.

Against `CYCLE-6-DRIFT.md`'s hundred 90-day replicates:

| Asset  | from a 30-day calibration | brute force | ratio |
| ------ | ------------------------- | ----------- | ----- |
| eurusd | 4.5%                      | 4.0%        | 1.13  |
| gbpjpy | 19.9%                     | 18.4%       | 1.08  |
| btcusd | 66.6%                     | 75.6%       | 0.88  |
| spx    | 1.5%                      | 1.7%        | 0.88  |
| xauusd | 8.1%                      | 8.4%        | 0.96  |

**Hitting the budget costs one simulation, not a search.** The calibration is
homogeneous of degree one in `volatility`: every layer multiplies the base, the
duration modulator reads only intervals, and the arrival process normalises
magnitudes against their own running average before using them. Measured at a
factor of 3.7, the quantum ratio agreed with the factor to 1.2e-12 and the
diffusion rate with its square to 2.9e-15, with tie rate, mean interval and
kurtosis bit-identical.

`asset.test.ts` checks that against a **real recalibration** rather than against
the algebra. If the two ever diverge, the recorded lattice stops being the
lattice the definition produces and INV-009's promise goes with it.

## 4. What the work found

**A fit needs four turnovers of the asset's own volatility memory.** The first
acceptance run fitted every budget from twelve hours and produced a blue-chip
index diffusing 4.9 times its own budget. Measured on `spx`, whose cascade
remembers for 44 hours, against a 30-day reference:

| pooled span | turnovers | median | range        |
| ----------- | --------- | ------ | ------------ |
| 12 h        | 0.3       | 1.12   | 0.84 to 1.49 |
| 24 h        | 0.5       | 0.84   | 0.47 to 1.64 |
| 48 h        | 1.1       | 0.84   | 0.64 to 1.02 |
| 96 h        | 2.2       | 1.04   | 0.93 to 1.31 |
| 240 h       | 5.5       | 0.99   | 0.75 to 1.14 |

The error is **variance, not bias** — the medians wander either side of one and
never settle away from it — which is the harder failure to notice. Nothing was
biased in that first run; two short estimates missed in opposite directions and
multiplied. B-002 in a fourth guise, now a refusal with a measured constant.

**Registration honoured a display coarser than the lattice it had just cut.** An
operator asking for two decimals on an asset whose lattice needs five would have
produced a screen showing an unchanged price on a contract that moved — INV-009
intact and the display lying. Now derived by default, and refused when coarser.

**One planted defect was inert, and that was the right answer.** Removing the
explicit trait-bounds check from `rescaleCalibration` broke nothing, because
`personalityConfig` already enforces them on every path. The duplicate was
removed rather than the test loosened.

## 5. Every guard watched failing

Fifteen defects planted, each reverted immediately, each run against the tests
that should have caught it.

| Planted defect                                               | Caught by                 | Failures |
| ------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------- | -------- |
| Rescale leaves the diffusion rate unscaled                   | `asset.test.ts`           | 2        |
| Rescale carries the old display precision                    | `asset.test.ts`           | 1        |
| The diffusion rate averages the absolute move and squares it | `catalogue.stat.test.ts`  | 5        |
| Sampling ignores the spacing ceiling                         | `families.test.ts`        | 14       |
| The starting clustering ignores depth                        | `families.test.ts`        | 11       |
| Feasibility checks the easy corner                           | `families.test.ts`        | 1        |
| Times are sampled linearly                                   | `families.test.ts`        | 1        |
| Every trait compared on a linear axis                        | `differentiation.test.ts` | 2        |
| The differentiation guard admits everything                  | `differentiation.test.ts` | 3        |
| The fit runs on any span at all                              | `registration.test.ts`    | 1        |
| A coarser display is honoured                                | `registration.test.ts`    | 1        |
| The pre-rescale definition is registered                     | `registration.test.ts`    | 1        |
| Dispersion forgets the square root                           | `registration.test.ts`    | 2        |
| Siblings are the midpoint of the box, not a draw             | `families.test.ts`        | 1        |
| Every archetype box shrunk to 2% of its width                | the acceptance, below     | 2        |

Four of these were **uncaught on the first attempt**, and each pointed at a test
that was not discriminating:

- The feasibility test's box was infeasible at _both_ corners, so a check reading
  the wrong one still threw. It now has a ceiling of 14.6 at the easy corner and
  2.28 at the worst.
- The log-sampling test counted draws either side of the midpoint, which
  separates log-uniform from uniform by barely one standard error. It now asserts
  the sample mean, where the alternative sits nine away.
- The absolute-move plant was run against `dispersion.stat.test.ts`, which
  compares brute force against the _recorded_ evidence and therefore cannot see a
  change to the code that produces it. `catalogue.stat.test.ts` recalibrates, and
  catches it.
- The clone-catalogue plant survived `min(sampled) > max(control)` at 35.7%
  against 34.8%. The real separation is eleven points; the assertion now requires
  five.

## 6. Acceptance

`tools/sim/src/sampledCatalogue.stat.test.ts` — 24 assets, three from each
archetype, each through the full registration.

**Every one of them is structurally sign-blind.** These personalities were drawn
by a generator rather than reasoned about by anyone, which is exactly why each
gets a mirror test: negate the sign stream and every latent variable must be
bit-identical while every increment is exactly negated. 24 of 24, zero
divergences.

**They land on the budgets their families declared.** Re-measured on an unrelated
keyring over a span the registration never saw: median ratio **1.156**, range
0.609 to 1.742, rank correlation between budget and realised **0.943** across a
range of budgets spanning a factor of forty.

**They separate from their own siblings.** Shape features only — pace and scale
removed — so nothing is won by moving more or ticking faster:

| stream family | three sampled siblings | three clones of one personality |
| ------------- | ---------------------- | ------------------------------- |
| signatures-a  | 47.8%                  | 31.0%                           |
| signatures-b  | 46.0%                  | 34.9%                           |
| signatures-c  | 47.8%                  | 33.8%                           |

Chance is 33.3%. The null is the **control's own distribution**, not a p-value:
Cycle Audit 4 measured a permutation null returning p ≤ 0.01 for five copies of
one personality in three of eight stream families, because each asset's windows
are contiguous slices of one realisation and share slow state. The control
carries every one of those dependences, because it is the same measurement with
the personalities made identical.

Across the whole 24, shape accuracy is 15.1–16.5% against a 4.2% chance rate.

`packages/engine/src/dispersion.stat.test.ts` closes the other side: forty
independent realisations per asset, the spread of where the price actually ends
up against what the calibration predicted. Pooled ratio **1.007** over three
assets, individual 0.940 to 1.060.
