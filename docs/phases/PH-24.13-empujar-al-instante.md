## 1. Objective

_"El empujar se torna demasiado lento; tiene que ser más dinámico e
instantáneo."_ Measured on EUR/USD (mean interval ~1.4 s, base tempo 2.2–4.2 s):
a push starts only after the already-drawn pending tick publishes — up to one
natural interval of waiting — and then advances one tick per natural interval,
so `+10` takes tens of seconds. That is the market's pace, faithfully, and it
is not the control the Human Owner described: _"clico y decido"_.

Two things make a push instantaneous without touching what a tick is:

1. **The pending tick is retracted.** A hosted market holds one drawn,
   unpublished tick. Nothing observed it; retracting it is not a rewrite of any
   record (INV-008, INV-009 concern what was published). The engine returns to
   the state from before that draw and draws again — same keystream positions,
   same magnitudes, the Lab's signs and arrivals. The first pushed tick is then
   the tick after the last **published** one, and its instant is the last
   published instant plus a burst interval, which is already in the past: it
   publishes on the next scheduler pass, which the push wakes immediately.
2. **The burst.** The engine's arrivals are a Hawkes process: an interval is
   `-ln(u) · base / intensity` for a uniform draw `u`. The Lab selects the
   arrival draws as it selects the signs — `SelectableArrival`, the same
   lockstep wrapper — and plays `u = e^(-1/12)` for the pushed ticks: intervals
   of one twelfth of the base tempo (~200–350 ms on EUR/USD), shorter still as
   the burst excites the process. Duration coupling makes fast ticks smaller
   (factor `(1/12)^0.18…0.32 ≈ 0.45–0.65`), exactly as the engine makes any
   fast tick smaller: a `+10` moves roughly five normal ticks in two to three
   seconds. After the burst the excitation decays as it always does.

Magnitudes are never touched. The mirror argument holds on the burst: the same
push with every sign flipped has identical intervals and identical magnitudes.

## 2. What is built

- `packages/runtime`: `HostedMarketOptions.retractable`; a market built
  retractable keeps the engine snapshot from before each draw and offers
  `retractPending()`, which restores it and clears the pending tick, or returns
  `false` when nothing can be retracted (nothing pending, or a pending tick
  inherited across a restart, whose pre-draw state this process never had).
  `ResumeOptions.arrivalSource` beside `signSource`; both optional, both
  absent by default.
- `apps/api`: `AppModuleOptions.arrivalSource`, passed through untouched
  (`composition.test.ts` extended); `VenueService` builds markets retractable
  only when Lab-composed; `labFork` takes an arrival wrapper too;
  `VenueService.wake()` reschedules the next pass immediately.
- `apps/api/src/lab`: `SelectableArrival` and `ArrivalSelector`; the push
  retracts, arms signs and burst arrivals, computes the landing on a fork
  playing both, and wakes the scheduler. Extend and replace keep their meaning.
- The panel polls the Lab every second instead of every two.

## 3. What this subphase may not do

- Touch a magnitude, or publish a tick with an instant before the last
  published one.
- Retract anything that was published.
- Exist in the production composition (`composition.test.ts`,
  `labSurface.test.ts`).

## 4. Acceptance criteria

1. On a Lab-composed venue a push retracts the pending tick: the first pushed
   tick carries the retracted tick's sequence, and it publishes on the very next
   advance.
2. The pushed ticks' intervals are at most `base / 12`; their signs are the
   push's; the same push mirrored gives identical intervals and magnitudes.
3. After the push, both wrappers are transparent.
4. From the screen, `+5` reads landed within four seconds of the click.
5. Guards, plants, targeted gate.

**What the gate found before approval.** (1) `BURST_DRAW` used `Math.exp`;
the portability guard (CA7-14) refused it — a replay must reproduce this
constant — and it now uses the kernel's `exp`. (2) The strip's landing line was
shown only while a push ran, and a burst lands before the strip's next poll:
the announcement now stays on the strip. (3) The flow read the session
timeline one poll too early; it waits for the poll.

**What a burst costs, said plainly.** Duration coupling makes a fast tick
smaller (`(1/12)^0.18…0.32`): a `+10` lands in about a second and moves about
what five natural ticks did. If the Human Owner wants the old size at the new
speed, each button unit becomes two or three burst ticks (DECISION-LOG,
"Revisit if").
