## 1. A min–max is not a bound, and this project read one as a bound twice

`DISPERSION_FIT_TURNOVERS = 4` was justified by a table of five seeds per span,
quoting the observed **range**. Range grows with sample size: five draws from a
16%-sd distribution span about ±25% by construction, and the stable statistic —
the standard deviation — was never quoted.

Re-derived with 24 seeds per span across three assets with very different
memories, four turnovers leaves a 16–34% standard deviation and puts three to six
seeds of twenty-four outside ±30%. **Sixteen** is where all three reach 24/24.

The same mistake appears in `differentiation.ts`'s threshold, which was set from
one 96-asset draw's minimum. It is set from **forty** independent catalogues now.

## 2. The error was not variance, and the direction mattered

"The error is variance, not bias" was the docstring's claim. The medians do not
wander: they sit below one and climb monotonically, because a mean of squares
over few independent volatility epochs is strongly right-skewed.

Since the rescale factor is `budget / measured`, a median asset fitted at four
turnovers **overshot its declared budget by about 14%** — which is exactly
PH-17.2's own acceptance median of 1.156, recorded there as success. With sixteen
turnovers that median is **1.024**, and the rank correlation between budget and
realised rose from 0.943 to 0.971.

## 4. An evidence document whose runner is not in the tree is not evidence

Both Cycle 6 evidence documents named a script — `runner.mjs`,
`backfillScale.mjs` — that exists nowhere, and neither recorded its seed.
`tools/sim/src/dispersionEvidence.ts` is in the repository, builds with
everything else, records its seed, and measures **both** sides: what the
calibration reports and what the real engine on its published lattice does. Both
older documents now carry a note saying what can and cannot be re-executed.

## 5. What is only partly closed, and said so

A 30% error in `logVariancePerMs` passed the entire suite, because
`dispersion.stat.test.ts` compared brute force against the **hard-coded**
evidence and never executed the line that computes the rate. It calibrates
freshly now, at the span the turnover floor requires for each asset's own memory
rather than a fixed two days that was 1.1 turnovers for two of the three.

Its resolution is **±25%, not ±14%**, and the reason is recorded rather than
tuned away: `xauusd` reads 1.33 against a fresh calibration and 1.20 in the
independent 40-run evidence, and two runs agreeing on a 20–33% gap is a signal
whose cause is not known (B-029). CA6-17 is therefore partly closed.
