# PH-13.3 — Enforcement in the Venue, With INV-001 Preserved

Type: SUBPHASE TECHNICAL DOCUMENT
Identifier: PH-13.3
Parent: [PH-13](PH-13-operator-risk.md)
Status: APPROVED
Created: 2026-09-01

## 1. Objective

Let the venue decline a trade that would breach its exposure limit, and prove
that the price path cannot tell the difference.

## 2. This is the most dangerous change to INV-001 since the trading boundary

Every previous cycle could rely on a simple fact: **the venue had no economic
state to leak.** The engine could not see positions because positions did not
exist anywhere the engine could reach.

This subphase creates exactly such a quantity. Net exposure per settlement event
is, by construction, correlated with the operator's interest — it is the number
that says which resolution costs money. A price path that could observe it would
be able to prefer the cheaper outcome, and INV-001 would be gone.

## 3. Why the existing defences are not sufficient on their own

Cycle Audit 4 (M-4) demonstrated the failure mode directly. An auditor placed a
module-level channel in `packages/core/src/market/`, wrote it from
`packages/trading/src/settle.ts`, and read it into the engine through an alias
table and a computed property access. **It named no economic term.** The
vocabulary scan saw nothing; only the behavioural test caught the ungated
version.

So the guarantee this subphase needs cannot be lexical. It has to be the
demonstration PH-6 established for trading and PH-7 for distribution, extended
one step: **a venue enforcing limits produces a bit-identical tick stream to one
that is not.**

## 4. A limit is a refusal, never an adjustment

If exposure is too concentrated, the venue **declines the trade** and says so.

It does not move the price, shade the payout, delay the tick, or widen a spread.
Those would all be the operator's interest reaching the trader's terms through a
channel the product promises is blind — and each is a small, reasonable-looking
change of exactly the kind Cycle 1 recorded about the leverage effect.

A refusal is visible to the trader, auditable after the fact, and structurally
incapable of influencing a price that has already been published.

## 5. Ordering, which is the whole safety argument

The limiter sees a tick **after** the engine has produced it, and a contract
**before** it is accepted. It never sits between the engine and the record.

That is the same shape PH-12.3 used for the publication writer — see the record,
do not participate in producing it — and it is why the demonstration can be
bit-identity rather than a statistical comparison.

## 6. Scope

- An exposure limiter over an open book, refusing what would breach capacity.
- Its integration at the venue's trading boundary.
- The INV-001 demonstration: identical ticks with the limiter active and
  refusing, versus absent, across the catalogue.
- A limit shown actually refusing, since one that never binds is theatre.

## 7. Exclusions

- Payout adjustment. Excluded by PH-13 §4 and restated here because it is the
  tempting alternative to refusing.
- Account balances, margin, custody. The limiter reasons about exposure, not
  about who owns what.

## 8. Acceptance criteria

1. A book within capacity is accepted; one that would breach it is refused, with
   the breach reported.
2. A refusal names the settlement event and the limit, so a trader can see why.
3. Netting is respected: a contract that _reduces_ net exposure is accepted even
   when the event is already at its limit.
4. Ticks are bit-identical between a venue with the limiter enforcing and one
   without it, on every catalogue asset.
5. The limiter cannot reach the engine, and the engine cannot reach the limiter —
   enforced by the dependency guard, not by inspection.

### The result

**All five catalogue assets produce bit-identical ticks with the limiter
actively accepting and refusing.** The test asserts the limiter was exercised in
both directions — more than 10 accepted and more than 100 refused per asset —
because a blindness demonstration against a limiter that never refuses anything
proves nothing.

This is stronger than PH-6's demonstration. That one showed identical ticks
between a quiet market and one under heavy adversarial trading, but trading could
only _add_ contracts. Here the venue **makes decisions from exposure**, reading
on every tick the quantity INV-001 says the price path must never see, and the
market is unchanged byte for byte.

### Against the acceptance criteria

1. Within capacity accepted, breaching refused, with the breach reported.
2. The refusal names the asset, the settlement window and the limit.
3. **Netting respected**: a contract on the opposite side of an event already
   over its limit is accepted, because refusing it would push the book further
   from balance while believing it was being careful.
4. Bit-identical ticks on every asset, with a teeth test proving the comparison
   can fail on a one-step difference.
5. `@otc/trading` depends on `@otc/core` alone; the engine cannot reach the
   limiter and the limiter holds no engine.

### What the subphase learned

**A performance defect surfaced the way they should — a test that could not
finish.** `admit` over a plain array recomputes the entire book's exposure on
every call: O(n) per admission, O(n²) per session. A market ticking every 333 ms
with thousands of open contracts would have spent its time re-deriving what it
already knew. `ExposureBook` maintains per-event exposure incrementally, and the
demonstration went from timing out at two minutes to 2.3 seconds.

The stateless functions were kept for one-off analysis rather than deleted,
because they are the readable statement of what the incremental version must
agree with.

**Netting is the subtle half of a limiter.** The naive rule — refuse anything
that touches an event at its limit — is worse than no limiter for the case that
matters, because it blocks precisely the hedges that would restore balance. The
rule is "refuse what _worsens_ net exposure", and that distinction is a test.

**The guarantee had to be behavioural, and Cycle Audit 4 is why.** That audit
defeated the vocabulary scan with neutral naming — a module-level channel written
from `settle.ts` and read into the engine through an alias table, naming no
economic term. A lexical guarantee here would have been worth nothing; comparing
bytes is worth something.

### Known limitations carried forward

- The limit is a single number per venue. A real operator would want it per
  asset, and per trader.
- Nothing expires contracts out of the book, so exposure only accumulates. A
  running venue needs settled events removed, which is where the book meets the
  settlement path.
