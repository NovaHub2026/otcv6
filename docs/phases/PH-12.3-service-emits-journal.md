# PH-12.3 — The Service Emits the Journal, and the Venue Gets a Policy

Type: SUBPHASE TECHNICAL DOCUMENT
Identifier: PH-12.3
Parent: [PH-12](PH-12-verifiable-publication.md)
Status: APPROVED
Created: 2026-09-01

---

## 1. Objective

Have the running venue actually produce what PH-12.1 and PH-12.2 built, and give
the catch-up bound the owner it has been missing since PH-5.

## 2. Two limitations, both carried for a while

**The journal is not emitted by anything.** PH-9.3 built a verdict a counterparty
can recompute from a published journal and recorded, as a limitation, that no
journal is produced by the service — everything it verifies is verified against
journals written by tests. A commitment scheme over a record nothing publishes is
a scheme over nothing.

**The catch-up bound is a default, not a policy.** Carried since PH-5:
_"a default with defined behaviour, not a decided venue policy. It needs an owner
before a real venue runs."_ Under ADR-0008 that owner is this subphase.

## 3. The commitment publisher

A `CommitmentPublisher` sits beside the venue and consumes what it publishes. It
holds no engine, reads no configuration, and cannot generate — it sees ticks
after they exist.

**Windows close on sequence count, not on wall-clock.** A window that closed on
elapsed time would produce windows of wildly different sizes across assets that
tick between 333 ms and 3.4 s, and would make the commitment cadence a function
of how busy the market is — which is exactly when an operator would most want to
delay. A fixed number of ticks makes the cadence a property of the record.

The last, partial window is **not** committed. Committing it and then extending
it would produce two different roots for overlapping ranges, and a verifier
cannot tell that from an operator restating history. A tick is committed when its
window is full, and until then it is published but uncommitted — which is the
honest state and one a counterparty can see.

## 4. The catch-up decision

When a venue is behind — restarted, paused, descheduled — it must decide how much
market time to generate at once.

**Decision: the bound stays, at 60 seconds, and it is now a stated policy rather
than a default.** Exceeding it is a refusal, not a silent fast-forward.

The alternative is to generate whatever is missing. That is rejected because a
long gap means the venue does not know what happened; generating hours of market
in a burst produces ticks whose instants are historical but whose _arrival_ is
not, and every observer who was watching during the gap saw nothing. Refusing
makes the operator confront a real outage. Fast-forwarding hides it inside a
plausible-looking price series, which is the worse failure for a market whose
entire product is a record people settle against.

Sixty seconds is chosen to absorb a process restart and a scheduler hiccup — the
things that happen normally — without absorbing an outage.

## 5. Scope

- `CommitmentPublisher`: fixed-size windows, signed roots, an append-only chain.
- Journal emission alongside the commitments, in the format `@otc/lab` reads.
- The venue's catch-up bound recorded as policy, with its rationale.
- End-to-end: run a venue, emit, and verify inclusion of an arbitrary tick from
  the published artefacts alone.

## 6. Exclusions

- Where roots are published. Making them publishable is this phase; choosing a
  channel is distribution.
- Retention and pruning of journals.

## 7. Acceptance criteria

1. A publisher fed a venue's output produces a chain that `verifySignedChain`
   accepts.
2. Windows tile the sequence space with no gap or overlap, across restarts.
3. A tick inside a closed window has a verifiable inclusion proof; a tick in the
   open window is reported as uncommitted rather than proved or denied.
4. The emitted journal is readable by `@otc/lab`'s reader and yields the same
   prices as the feed.
5. The catch-up policy is stated where it is enforced, and its refusal is tested.

---

## 8. Subphase approval record

**APPROVED** from executed evidence, 2026-09-01.

### The result

A counterparty holding only the published directory and a public key can
establish that a specific tick was in the operator's committed record —
`publication.stat.test.ts`, seven tests, no engine and no private key at
verification time.

**PH-9.3's carried limitation is closed.** It built a verdict recomputable from a
journal and recorded that nothing produced one; every check it ran was against
journals written by tests.

### Against the acceptance criteria

1. **A chain `verifySignedChain` accepts** — produced by a real engine through a
   real writer, read back off disk.
2. **Windows tile with no gap or overlap** — asserted across restarts, including
   the case where the open window is lost and the resumed publisher starts after
   the last _committed_ sequence rather than folding orphans into a later window
   whose range would then be a lie.
3. **Committed ticks provable, open-window ticks reported as uncommitted** —
   both, and the second is stated as an honest third state rather than a proof or
   a denial.
4. **The journal is readable by `@otc/lab`** — by the real reader, which refuses
   gaps, reordering and truncation. `@otc/api` cannot depend on lab, so this is
   the only way the format claim could be checked rather than asserted.
5. **The catch-up policy is stated where it is enforced** — ADR-0010, with its
   refusal tested.

### What the subphase learned

**Where the writer lives was decided by what could test it.** The first version
put journal and commitment writing inside the NestJS service. Nothing in it is
NestJS-specific, and the consequence was worse than untidy: `@otc/api` cannot
depend on `@otc/lab`, so the format-compatibility claim could only have been
checked against a reimplementation of the reader — which checks nothing.

Moving it to `@otc/distribution` let `tools/sim` verify the artefacts with the
real reader. The architecture rule did not just permit the better design, it
pointed at it.

**One journal per commitment window fell out of the format.** The journal header
carries its tick count, so it cannot be appended to without rewriting the header
— and a record whose header is rewritten as it grows is one an operator can
quietly reshape. A window is complete when committed, so pairing them one-to-one
makes the archival unit and the verification unit the same thing.

**The catch-up bound had been undecided for seven phases.** PH-5 wrote it down as
a policy question and set an hour so the runtime would have defined behaviour.
Deciding it (ADR-0010) broke 31 tests, all of which advanced the clock 60 to 600
seconds in one step — relying on the production default to permit exactly what
production should not permit. They now declare their bursts.

### Known limitations carried forward

- Where roots are published is unaddressed. Making them publishable was the
  scope; choosing a channel — a transparency log, a chain, a mirror — is a
  distribution decision.
- No retention or pruning policy for journals.
- Key rotation is supported by the format but has no procedure.
