## 1. Objective

A commitment over the published record that lets a counterparty prove one tick
was in it, without holding the whole record and without trusting the operator at
verification time.

## 2. Where it lives, and why not next to the journal

The journal is in `@otc/lab`, which would be the obvious home. It is the wrong
one: `@otc/api` cannot depend on `@otc/lab` — the dependency allowlist forbids it,
correctly, because lab pulls in the planted-defect fixture corpus and a running
venue has no business carrying that.

The commitment goes in **`@otc/distribution`**, which is where the published
record already lives and which `@otc/api` already depends on. It is defined over
`Tick` — the core type, the published triple — rather than over `TickJournal`, so
it commits to exactly what observers see and needs nothing from lab.

`apps/web` does not depend on distribution, so `node:crypto` is available here.

## 3. Construction

```
leaf  = SHA256( 0x00 || sequence || instant || price )
node  = SHA256( 0x01 || left || right )
root  = SHA256( 0x02 || assetId || from || to || count || previousRoot || merkle )
```

Every design choice below closes a specific known attack.

### 3.1 Domain separation is not decoration

Leaves and internal nodes carry different prefixes. Without that, an attacker can
present an **internal node** as though it were a leaf: the verifier hashes it,
gets a value already in the tree, and the proof checks out. It is the classic
second-preimage attack on Merkle trees, and the only defence is that a leaf hash
and a node hash can never collide by construction.

### 3.2 Odd nodes are promoted, never duplicated

Bitcoin duplicates the final node when a level has an odd count, which is
CVE-2012-2459: two different leaf sets produce the same root, so a commitment
does not uniquely identify a record.

Here an odd final node is **carried up unchanged**, and the leaf `count` is bound
into the root. A verifier replays the promotion using the count, so a tree of a
different size cannot produce a matching root.

### 3.3 The root binds more than the leaves

Asset, sequence range, count and the previous root all enter the final hash.

- **Asset** — a proof for one market must not verify against another's root.
- **Range** — a root states exactly which sequences it covers, so ranges can be
  checked to tile without gaps or overlap.
- **Previous root** — the chain is append-only. Rewriting any earlier window
  invalidates every root after it, so an operator cannot quietly restate history.

## 4. What it proves

That a tick was in the committed record, and that the record was not altered
after the root was published.

**It does not prove the market was generated fairly.** That rests on ADR-0003's
theorem, the mirror test and the attack battery — all of which a counterparty
re-runs themselves. The verifier's output must say so, because a cryptographic
result reads as stronger than it is and the temptation to let it stand for the
statistical one is the main rhetorical risk of this phase.

## 5. Acceptance criteria

1. Deterministic: the same ticks produce the same root, across processes.
2. Every leaf has a proof that verifies.
3. Altering any field of any tick invalidates its proof.
4. An internal node presented as a leaf does not verify (§3.1).
5. Two different leaf sets never share a root, including at odd counts (§3.2).
6. A proof does not verify against another asset's root, or a different range.
7. Altering an earlier root invalidates the chain from that point.
8. Guardrails, lint, build and the unit suite pass.

`commitment.test.ts` contributes 40 tests, most of them attacks rather than
behaviour: a tree that only ever sees valid input proves nothing about what it
does with invalid input, and invalid input is the entire threat model.

### Against the acceptance criteria

1. **Deterministic** — same ticks, same root.
2. **Every leaf has a proof** — verified exhaustively at counts 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 7,
   8, 9, 16, 17, 31, 64 and 100, which covers powers of two, odd levels and the
   promotion path.
3. **Tampering invalidates** — price, instant, index, sibling, truncated path,
   and a path with junk appended.
4. **Second preimage** — an internal node offered as a leaf does not verify, and
   the test asserts the tags differ rather than trusting that they do.
5. **CVE-2012-2459** — 40 leaf counts, no two share a root; a duplicated tail
   does not forge a match.
6. **Asset and range** — different assets and different ranges over the same
   prices produce different roots.
7. **Chain** — a rewritten window is reported at the link where it breaks, along
   with gaps, count/range disagreement, asset switches, and an empty chain.

### What the subphase learned

**The first version of these tests asserted a property the construction does not
have, and should not have.** Two tests expected an inclusion proof to fail
against another asset's commitment, and against a re-anchored one. They failed —
because a proof is a leaf and a path, carrying no asset and no chain position, so
it verifies against _any_ commitment that genuinely covers those ticks at that
index.

That is correct. The two properties live in different layers: **the root** binds
asset, range, count and predecessor so two records can never share one; **the
chain** binds position, because `verifyChain` is the only thing that can see the
neighbouring windows. Expecting `verifyInclusion` to catch re-anchoring puts the
check in the one layer with no view of what it is checking against.

The tests now assert the real property and the layering is documented at the
function, because the mistake was in my model of the design rather than in the
design.

**Three published attacks were closed by construction, not by review.** Domain
separation between leaves and nodes; promotion instead of duplication for odd
levels, with the count bound into the root; and full framing of asset, range and
predecessor. Each is a specific, named, historical break of a Merkle scheme, and
each has a test that would fail if the defence were removed.

### Known limitations carried forward

- Nothing is signed yet. A commitment identifies a record; it does not yet
  establish **who** committed to it. PH-12.2.
- No producer emits commitments; they are computed by tests. PH-12.3.
