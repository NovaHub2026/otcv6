## 1. Objective

Move the market's time structure out of shared defaults and into the personality
vector, without weakening the analytic kurtosis gate and without changing the one
configuration a full battery has cleared.

## 2. What is shared today, and what it costs

`personalityConfig` inherits `components`, `slowestHazardPerMs` and `hazardRatio`
from `DEFAULT_CASCADE`, every regime sojourn scale from `DEFAULT_REGIMES`, and
`decayPerMs` from `DEFAULT_HAWKES`. Those five quantities determine the timescales
on which volatility clusters and bursts decay — which is to say, they determine
five of the seven scale-free shape features the differentiation metric measures.
No asset can vary any of them. PH-10 §2.2 has the table.

## 3. The five new traits

| Trait             | Meaning                                                | Default (reproduces today exactly) |
| ----------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------- |
| `cascadeDepth`    | Number of cascade components: how many timescales      | 10                                 |
| `cascadeSpanMs`   | Mean switching time of the **slowest** component       | 21_600_000 (6 h)                   |
| `cascadeSpacing`  | Geometric ratio between successive components' hazards | 2.6                                |
| `regimeTempo`     | Scalar on every regime sojourn scale                   | 1                                  |
| `arrivalMemoryMs` | Hawkes excitation memory: `decayPerMs = 1 / this`      | 120_000                            |

`cascadeSpanMs` and `arrivalMemoryMs` are expressed as **times**, not hazards,
because that is the quantity an author can reason about, and because
`1 / 21_600_000` and `1 / 120_000` reproduce the existing constants to the last
bit rather than to within an ulp.

## 4. The result that makes this safe

Kurtosis is `3 · cascadeInflation · regimeInflation · structureInflation − 3`,
and the new traits enter it as follows:

- `cascadeSpanMs`, `cascadeSpacing` — **not at all.** `cascadeInflation` depends
  only on the component multiplier and the component count. Rescaling _when_
  components switch cannot change the moments of their product.
- `regimeTempo` — **not at all, and this is a theorem rather than a rounding
  observation.** `regimeInflation` weights each regime by its stationary time
  share, computed as `embedded · meanSojourn` normalised by its own total.
  Multiplying every sojourn scale by a common factor multiplies numerator and
  denominator alike; it cancels exactly.
- `arrivalMemoryMs` — **not at all.** Arrival timing is not a magnitude
  multiplier and appears nowhere in the inflation product.
- `cascadeDepth` — as an **exponent**. `cascadeInflation = inflation(c) ^ depth`.

So four of the five new rhythm traits are kurtosis-neutral and the fifth is the
one that has already cost this project a recalibration. That asymmetry is the
whole design: rhythm can be varied freely, and depth is varied together with
clustering.

**Two of those neutralities are different in kind, and the tests treat them
differently.** `cascadeSpanMs`, `cascadeSpacing` and `arrivalMemoryMs` are
neutral by **absence** — they are read nowhere in the moment product, so the
computation is bit-for-bit identical and the assertion is `toBe`. `regimeTempo`
is neutral by **cancellation**, which is exact in real arithmetic and ulp-scale
in floating point: `(scale · tempo) · Γ` and `tempo · (scale · Γ)` are not the
same double, so the ratio comes back a few ulps apart. Measured: 3 ulps, a
relative 5e-16.

The distinction is worth the extra test because the two claims fail differently.
An `absence` claim that starts drifting means someone wired a hazard into the
tail; a `cancellation` claim that drifts by more than ulps means the tempo
stopped being common to every regime. `toBe` on the first three would have
caught the first; `toBe` on `regimeTempo` would simply have been false.

## 5. The co-varied solve

`solveClustering(traits, targetExcessKurtosis, stream)` returns the `clustering`
that makes `predictedExcessKurtosis` equal the target, holding every other trait
fixed.

It is cheap because of §4. The regime and structure inflations do not depend on
clustering at all, so they are computed **once**, and the search runs over the
closed form

```
inflation(c) = (1 + 6c² + c⁴) / (1 + c²)²
```

which is the two-point multiplier's `E[M⁴]/E[M²]²` written in terms of
`c = clustering` (with `low = 1 − c`, `high = 1 + c`). It is strictly increasing
on `[0, 1)`, so bisection converges monotonically and needs no root selection.

Bisection rather than the quadratic's closed form is deliberate: the quadratic
has a sign flip at `t = 1` and two roots of which only one is admissible, and a
root-selection bug there would be silent. Bisection on a monotone function cannot
select the wrong root.

The solve **throws** rather than clamping when the target is unreachable —
either because the required cascade inflation is below 1 (the other layers alone
already exceed the target) or because the required clustering exceeds its bound.
A clamped solve would silently hand back a personality that misses its target,
which is the failure mode the analytic gate exists to prevent.

### 5.1 The stream is load-bearing

`structureInflation` has no closed form — the structural phase hazard depends on
phase age and on how compressed the path has been — so it is estimated by
simulating that layer alone. `predictedExcessKurtosis` is therefore a
**stream-dependent estimate**, and the solve is exact only with respect to the
stream it was handed.

Measured: solving against one stream and evaluating against another lands within
about 1% of the target rather than at 1e-9. That is the structure estimator's own
noise, not a defect in the solve, but it has a consequence for PH-10.2 — a
catalogue must derive this stream from a **recorded label** and record the
**achieved** kurtosis rather than the requested one. Recording the request would
be publishing a number the project never computed.

This was found by a test that used two different stream labels by accident. It
is now a test on purpose: the same-stream error must be under 1e-9 and the
cross-stream error must be **above** it, so a future change that made the solve
silently stream-independent — by sampling fewer steps, say — would fail rather
than look like an improvement.

## 6. Scope

- Five traits, their bounds, and their expansion.
- `cascadeInflationOfClustering` and `solveClustering`.
- `cascadeRmsGain`, so an author can see the scale change a depth change causes.
- A joint bound rejecting a cascade whose fastest component is faster than the
  market can show: `MIN_FASTEST_COMPONENT_TICKS`.
- Tests: exact reproduction of today's configuration, the neutrality claims of
  §4 asserted rather than argued, solve correctness and its refusals, and
  sign-blindness of everything added.

## 6.1 The joint bound

A component survives a tick with probability `exp(−tempo / switchingTime)`. Below
half a tick that is under 13.5%: the component is independent at every observable
lag, so it pays its full share of kurtosis — depth is an exponent — and buys no
autocorrelation, which is the only reason it exists.

The bound is relative to `tempoMs` rather than absolute, because "too fast to
see" is a statement about the observer: 500 ms is a rhythm in a market that ticks
every three seconds and noise in one that ticks every 300.

It is a floor on **waste**, not on safety, and that is exactly why it has to be
loud. `solveClustering` would otherwise absorb the wasted kurtosis by thinning
every component — including the slow ones that carry the market's long memory —
so the symptom of a badly-shaped ladder is not an error but a market with
quietly weaker structure. The default personality sits at 1.59 ticks.

Every trait involved can be individually in range while the combination is
degenerate, so this cannot live in the per-trait bounds loop.

## 7. Exclusions

- Re-authoring the catalogue — PH-10.2.
- Any revalidation run — PH-10.3.

## 8. Acceptance criteria

1. `expandPersonality(DEFAULT_TRAITS, …)` is still byte-identical to
   `DEFAULT_ENGINE_CONFIG`.
2. Each neutrality claim in §4 is a test: varying the trait leaves
   `predictedExcessKurtosis` **exactly** unchanged where §4 says exactly.
3. `solveClustering` hits its target to within 1e-9, across depths 4…18, with
   respect to the stream it was given — and demonstrably not across streams.
4. It throws, with a diagnostic naming the cause, on both unreachable directions.
5. The mirror test still passes exactly, on a personality using the new traits.
6. Guardrails, lint, build and the unit suite pass.

### Against the acceptance criteria

1. **Exact reproduction** — `personalityConfig(DEFAULT_TRAITS)` returns the
   hand-written hazard, decay and every regime sojourn scale bit for bit. The
   pre-existing `toEqual` on the whole config object passes, and `rhythm.test.ts`

   two values that had drifted together.
2. **Neutrality** — asserted, and split in two. Bit-exact for the three traits
   that are neutral by absence; within 5e-16 for `regimeTempo`, which is neutral

3. **The solve** — hits its target to within 1e-9 at every depth from 4 to 18 and
   at targets from 12 to 150.
4. **Refusals** — three, each with a diagnostic naming the cause: a target the

   target that is not a positive finite number.
5. **The mirror test** — exact on a personality using all five new traits: zero
   divergences at burn-ins of 700, 9,000, 20,000 and 35,000 ticks.
6. **Quality gate** — above.

### What the subphase learned

**"Exactly neutral" was two claims wearing one word.** Three of the four
neutralities are bit-exact, because the traits are read nowhere in the moment
product. `regimeTempo` is not: it cancels in real arithmetic, and in floating
point `(scale · tempo) · Γ` and `tempo · (scale · Γ)` land 3 ulps apart. The
first draft asserted `toBe` on all four and failed on the fourth — which was the
test doing its job on the documentation rather than on the code. They now get
different assertions, because they would fail for different reasons.

**The solve is exact only against the stream it was given.** `structureInflation`
has no closed form and is estimated by simulation, so `predictedExcessKurtosis`
is stream-dependent. The first version of the test solved with one stream label
and verified with another; it missed by a uniform 0.69% at every depth, which is
the signature of a constant offset rather than a search that fails to converge.
The consequence is a rule for PH-10.2: an asset records the kurtosis it
**achieved**, computed from a recorded stream label, never the one it requested.

**A joint bound was needed, and the first version of it was wrong.** A fastest
component that resamples between ticks pays full kurtosis for no autocorrelation
— and `solveClustering` would hide the cost by thinning every component,
including the slow ones that carry the long memory. So the waste has to be an
error. The first bound was absolute (100 ms), which is meaningless without an
observer: 500 ms is a rhythm at a three-second tick and noise at a 300 ms one.
The bound is now relative to `tempoMs`, at the point where a component survives a
tick with probability `exp(−2)`. The calibrated default sits at 1.59 ticks —
admissible, and not by much, which is the honest reading of how little
fast-component headroom PH-3 actually left.

### Known limitations carried forward

- The trait space is now geometrically constrained in a way no single bound
  expresses: `(depth − 1) · ln(spacing) ≤ ln(span / (tempo/2))`. Deep ladders
  require tight spacing. The error message names the three traits involved, but
  an author still has to solve it by hand.
- `predictedExcessKurtosis` remains a simulation estimate through its structure
  term. PH-10.2 must record achieved values against a recorded stream.
