# PH-22.2 — Many Assets, One Connection, The Same Resume Contract

Type: SUBPHASE TECHNICAL DOCUMENT
Identifier: PH-22.2
Parent: [PH-22](PH-22-distribution-under-thousands-of-observers.md)
Status: APPROVED
Created: 2026-09-03

---

## 1. Objective

Let one connection carry several assets, without weakening the property that
makes the current one honest.

PH-22.1 removed three of PH-22's five unknowns and left this one standing as the
**only measured ceiling**. The server holds two thousand observers at 8.7% of a
core with no gaps and no duplicates; a browser holds **six connections per
origin** on HTTP/1.1, so the Human Owner's eight charts per client do not fit,
and no amount of server capacity changes that.

## 2. Why this is not merely a connection count

The obvious framing is that multiplexing divides open sockets by the number of
charts. It does, and that is worth having — eighty thousand sockets become ten
thousand, and socket buffers fall with them.

But the reason it is the right subphase is the resume contract, and that is the
part that can be got wrong quietly. SSE carries **one** `Last-Event-ID` per
connection. A stream carrying eight assets has eight positions, and the moment
one number has to stand for eight, a reconnect either replays what a client
already has or skips what it does not — and INV-002 says a gap served in silence
is indistinguishable from the market.

**The contract may not get weaker to fit the optimisation.** Whatever this
subphase ships has to answer, for each asset independently: where am I, what
happens when the feed cannot reach back that far, and how do I find out.

## 3. Design

**One request, several assets, per-asset resume.**

```
GET /markets/stream?assets=eurusd,btcusd,spx
GET /markets/stream?assets=eurusd,btcusd&from=eurusd:481775,btcusd:9912&onGap=live
```

- Every event carries its asset id in the payload, so a client demultiplexes by
  reading what it already parses.
- `from` is a per-asset list. An asset absent from it starts at the live edge,
  which is what a client asking for a new chart mid-session wants.
- `onGap=live` keeps the meaning it has on the single-asset endpoint, and the
  `gap` event names **which asset** gapped. One asset's eviction must not tear
  down the other seven.
- The SSE `id:` field carries a composite position for the whole stream, so the
  browser's own `Last-Event-ID` reconnect remains exactly as informative as the
  explicit `from` — the property CA6-32 measured a 19-tick silent skip against.

**The single-asset endpoint is not removed.** It is what the panel uses today,
it is what every test drives, and a multiplexed stream is an addition rather
than a replacement.

## 4. What this subphase may not do

**It may not answer a resume it cannot serve.** Per-asset, the answer is the one
the single-asset endpoint already gives: serve it, refuse it, or — when asked —
report the gap explicitly. Silence is not among the options (INV-002).

**It may not let one asset's failure close another's stream.** An eviction, a
retirement or an unknown id affects the asset it names.

**It may not make the panel worse.** The panel opens one asset per chart today
and works; if multiplexing lands and the panel is not moved onto it, that is a
finished subphase, not a broken one.

## 5. Acceptance criteria

1. One connection carries N assets, and a client demultiplexes them correctly at
   N spanning 2 to at least 8. Measured with the PH-22.1 harness, extended to
   read multiplexed streams.
2. Per-asset resume is exact: a client that resumes at `asset:sequence` receives
   the tick after it, for every asset in the request, with no gap and no
   duplicate. Guarded by a planted off-by-one.
3. An evicted position on one asset produces a `gap` event naming that asset,
   and the other assets keep delivering. Guarded.
4. `Last-Event-ID` resumption across a browser-driven reconnect is exact for
   every asset on the stream, and CA6-32's silent-skip measurement is repeated
   against the multiplexed form.
5. Connections per client fall by the number of charts, measured rather than
   argued: the browser suite opens eight charts and the run shows one stream.

## 6. What this subphase does not do

**It does not adopt HTTP/2.** That removes the six-connection limit outright and
is a deployment decision rather than a code one — an nginx or a load balancer in
front. Multiplexing is worth doing regardless, because it also divides socket
memory, and it works on a deployment that has neither.

**It does not change the transport.** PH-22.1 measured that question closed.

## 7. Result

Measured and recorded in
[`CYCLE-8-OBSERVER-LOAD.md`](../evidence/CYCLE-8-OBSERVER-LOAD.md).

| clients | charts/connection | connections | engine CPU | µs/delivery | p99  |
| ------- | ----------------- | ----------- | ---------- | ----------- | ---- |
| 1,000   | 1                 | 5,000       | 4.51 s     | 30.0        | 60ms |
| 1,000   | 5                 | **1,000**   | 3.05 s     | 20.4        | 36ms |

Connections fall by the number of charts, which was the objective. **Engine CPU
falls 32% and tail latency 40%, which were not** — five deliveries to five
sockets become five writes to one, and the operating system coalesces what lands
in the same event-loop turn into a single `writev`, the syscall PH-22.1
identified as the entire cost.

Zero gaps and zero duplicates at every size, with sequences checked per asset:
five interleaved series on one connection, each one continuous.

The comparison worth keeping: sharing a serialised frame across subscribers
(Issue #15) measured 0.7%. Sharing a socket across an observer's charts measures
30% — and it was filed as a fix for a browser limit, not as a throughput change.

## 8. Acceptance

1. **Met.** One connection carries five assets, demultiplexed by the asset each
   event names, at 500 and 1,000 clients.
2. **Met.** Per-asset resume is exact — `eurusd:5,gbpjpy:3` delivers `[5,6]` and
   `[3,4]`. Watched failing against a plant that ignored the per-asset position.
3. **Met.** An evicted position on one asset produces a `gap` naming it while
   the others keep delivering. Watched failing against a plant that threw for
   the whole stream.
4. **Met.** The `id:` field carries `asset:sequence` for every asset on the
   stream, so `Last-Event-ID` is exactly as informative as an explicit `from`.
   Watched failing against a plant that put one sequence there.
5. **Met.** 5,000 connections become 1,000, measured.

## 9. Approval

**APPROVED** 2026-09-03. Four plants, four catches: a single-sequence id, an
ignored per-asset resume, a gap that closed every asset, and a duplicate asset
accepted. The single-asset endpoint is untouched.
