# PH-11.1 — Is the Independent Error Bar Honest?

Type: SUBPHASE TECHNICAL DOCUMENT
Identifier: PH-11.1
Parent: [PH-11](PH-11-detection-power.md)
Status: APPROVED
Created: 2026-08-31

## 1. Objective

Decide, by measurement, whether the minimum detectable effect every verdict in
this project is quoted with is the right one — and build the instrument that
decides it.

## 2. Why this comes first

`minimumDetectableEffect(n)` is `(z_alpha + z_power) * sqrt(0.25 / n)`. That is a
formula for `n` **independent** trials. Every clean verdict the project has ever
produced is quoted with a floor computed this way: 0.217pp at 30 seconds in PH-3,
0.561pp per asset in PH-4, 0.561pp for the withheld families in PH-9.

PH-10 gave a concrete reason to doubt it. The realised lattice tie rate came back
carrying roughly **four times** its binomial variance, because whether a window
settles at the money depends on the volatility level, and volatility is
autocorrelated over days. Consecutive windows are not independent draws.

If direction behaved the same way, every floor in the project would be optimistic
and every "clean" verdict would overstate its own sensitivity. That is not a
detail — it is the number the whole anti-predictability claim is measured
against.

## 3. What the theory says, and why it is not enough

ADR-0003 gives `P(up) = 1/2` **exactly**, under every public conditioning,
whatever the volatility. Volatility clustering changes how _far_ a window moves;
it never changes which way. So non-overlapping direction outcomes should be
independent fair coins even though almost nothing else about this market is
independent.

That is an argument, and the project's own rule is that an argument is not
evidence. This subphase measures it, with an instrument capable of returning the
opposite answer.

## 4. The instrument

`designEffect(proportions, samplesPerReplicate)` runs the measurement as `R`
independent replicates of `n` trials, and compares the variance actually observed
across replicates with the `p(1 - p) / n` that independence predicts. Their ratio
is the design effect: 1 means the error bar is honest, 4 means the effective
sample size is a quarter of the nominal one.

It reports its own resolution. A design effect is a variance estimate and
variance estimates are noisy — at `R` replicates the ratio carries a relative
standard error of `sqrt(2 / (R - 1))`, which at 40 replicates is ±23%. Quoting a
ratio without that invites exactly the overconfidence the function exists to
prevent, so `relativeStandardError` is part of the result rather than a footnote.

`minimumDetectableEffectUnderDependence(trials, deff)` then divides the sample
size by the measured design effect. It floors the effect at 1: a measurement
below 1 is sampling noise, not a licence to claim more sensitivity than the
trials support.

## 5. The control that makes it worth something

An estimator that returned 1 unconditionally would satisfy every direction
assertion in this subphase.

So the tie rate is accumulated **from the same runs, the same windows and the
same ticks**, and it must come out clearly above 1. One pass, two statistics,
opposite expected answers. The estimator cannot satisfy both by being broken.

`dependence.test.ts` adds a second control that does not need the engine at all:
a beta-binomial series whose true design effect is `1 + (n - 1) * rho` in closed
form, so the estimator is checked against a number rather than against an
impression.

## 6. The streaming accumulator

Measuring eight horizons needs eight tilings of one price path.
`HorizonAccumulator` maintains a boundary and an open price per horizon and
classifies each window as it closes, in `O(horizons)` memory regardless of run
length.

It exists because of PH-11.2 — a 15-minute horizon at full power needs about
2×10⁸ ticks, which `ObserverDataset` would materialise as roughly 2.4 GB — but it
earns its place here by giving every horizon the same windows from one pass.

Two properties are asserted rather than assumed, because both are load-bearing
and neither is visible in the output:

- **Windows tile, they do not slide.** Overlapping windows share increments and
  would be dependent by construction, which is precisely what the design effect
  is trying to detect elsewhere.
- **A window closes at the last price strictly before its boundary** — the rule
  settlement uses, so a tally and a settlement can never disagree about which
  side a contract landed on.

## 7. Acceptance criteria

1. The design effect reads within its own resolution of 1 at every horizon the
   product sells.
2. The tie-rate control, from the same pass, reads clearly above 1 — and above
   the direction figure at the same horizon.
3. The estimator recovers a known closed-form design effect on a constructed
   series.
4. Honest floors are reported per horizon, carrying the measured design effect.
5. Guardrails, lint, build and the unit suite pass.

### Against the acceptance criteria

1. **Within resolution of 1 at every horizon** — yes. At 40 replicates the ratio
   carries a ±23% relative standard error, so the band is ±0.69 and the observed
   range is 0.77 to 1.62.
2. **The control reads above 1, and above direction at the same horizon** — 4.62
   against 0.89, a factor of 5.2.
3. **Closed-form recovery** — the estimator returns 1.80 on a constructed
   beta-binomial whose true design effect is `1 + (n − 1)ρ = 1.80`.
4. **Honest floors reported** — the table above, carrying the measured effect.
5. **Quality gate** — above.

### What the subphase established

**The i.i.d. floor is the right one for direction, and this was not obvious.**
PH-10 had just found the lattice tie rate carrying four times its binomial
variance. Applying that lesson uncritically to direction would have been the
natural move, and it would have been wrong — it would have inflated every floor
in the project by a factor that is not there.

The distinction is exact and worth keeping: a tie's probability _tracks the
volatility level_, which is autocorrelated over days, so tie outcomes are
overdispersed. A direction's probability is `1/2` **regardless of volatility**,
because clustering changes how far a window moves and never which way. One
statistic of this market is dependent and the other is not, and they are computed
from the same windows.

That is ADR-0003 showing up in a place nobody had looked for it.

**Two horizons closed as a side effect.** 30 seconds at 0.143pp and 1 minute at
0.204pp are both under the 0.2513pp the promotional payout implies — from 333
simulated days, which cost under a minute of compute. B-002 recorded the
15-minute horizon as needing "roughly a hundred times the history", which was
true and read as prohibitive. Measured first: the engine produces **730,638 ticks
per second**, so a simulated year costs about 31 seconds.

The real obstacle was never compute. It was that nobody had checked whether the
error bar was valid, and if it had not been, simulating longer would have bought
far less than the arithmetic promised.

### Known limitations carried forward

- ±23% is a wide resolution. The 10-minute horizon reads 1.62 — the largest of
  the eight, at 1.7 standard errors, which is unremarkable across eight readings
  but is not _nothing_. PH-11.2's long run splits into contiguous segments and
  will resolve exactly the long horizons where this measurement is loosest.
- The design effect is measured across independent runs. Contiguous segments of a
  single long record are the more realistic setting for a live venue, and PH-11.2
  measures that instead.
- `HorizonAccumulator` conditions on nothing. It answers one narrow question at a
  sample size the battery cannot reach, and it is **not** stronger evidence than
  the battery — it is different evidence.

## 10. Corrections from Cycle Audit 004

**The acceptance band could not see the value that would break the phase.** At 40
replicates the design-effect estimate carries a ±23% relative error, so the band
was [0.32, 1.68] — and a true `deff` of 1.5 makes every floor 1.22× coarser,
pushing **all five 15-minute cells across the payout threshold**. The guard would
have missed that 70% of the time. An acceptance band must be narrower than the
effect it excludes; this one was not. Replicates raised to 100.

**The estimator is structurally blind to a run-wide common component.** A
component shared by _every_ replicate is removed by the sample variance by
construction: measured, it reads 1.000 while the pooled statistic carries 11.6×
its assumed variance. So sharing across replicates _lowers_ the reading, and
PH-11.2's claim that contiguous segments are "the more conservative" measurement
is backwards. Worse, the invisible mode is exactly the path-displacement channel
PH-11.2 builds `pathBiasZ` around.

The conclusion is unaffected — the pooled z was confirmed honest across 400
genuinely independent realisations — but it was not this instrument that
established it, and the documents said it was.
