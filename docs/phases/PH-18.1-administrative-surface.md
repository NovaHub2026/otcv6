## 1. What a panel needs that did not exist

The venue could stream ticks and report where a market is now. Three things a
Preview screen needs were missing:

- **What kind of market is this.** `/markets` answers "where is it", which is the
  question a client asks. An operator asks what its pace is, how often it ties,
  how fat its tail is, and how far it diffuses in a quarter — all of it recorded
  at registration and none of it published.
- **What did it do before now.** PH-17.3 built the history tier; nothing served
  from it.
- **What can I make.** Eight archetypes, each a region with a dispersion band,
  and no way to see them.

Three read-only endpoints: `/catalogue`, `/archetypes`, `/markets/:id/history`.

## 2. The history is written by the venue and read by the panel

`HistoryService` does the same job at two speeds. Continuously, it folds every
published tick into minute and hour bars and flushes on the checkpoint cadence —
a minute bar closes at most once a minute, so flushing per tick would be
thousands of empty writes for each real one. Once, on request, it provisions a
market that has no record.

It observes the record **after** publication, exactly as `PublicationService`
does and for the same reason: a chart is a view of what happened, and a view that
could influence what happens next would be the whole product broken (INV-001).

## 3. Provisioning is off by default

`OTC_BACKFILL_DAYS` defaults to zero.

A backfill is genesis and refuses to run twice, so it is **irreversible**: the
history an asset gets is the first one it is given. Making an irreversible act
the default behaviour of a process start would let booting the service in the
wrong directory permanently decide what a market's past is.

It runs inside `VenueService.start`, before the markets resume, because the
checkpoint a backfill leaves is exactly what `resumeMarket` then continues from.
Putting it in the caller would make that ordering something a caller has to
remember. An asset that already has a record is skipped rather than refused: a
restart is the ordinary case and is not an error.

## 4. Refusals, not approximations

`/markets/:id/history` refuses a timeframe finer than the stored base rather than
returning a coarser series under the requested name. INV-004 says the displayed
timeframe never changes the market; it must not change what the market appears to
_have been_ either. A deployment that keeps no history at all answers with a 404
that says so, because an empty array reads as "this asset did nothing".

## 5. Every guard watched failing

Nine defects planted, each reverted immediately.

| Planted defect                                  | Failures |
| ----------------------------------------------- | -------- |
| The catalogue reports every asset as hosted     | 1        |
| A refused read is answered with an empty series | 1        |
| A window may end before it starts               | 2        |
| An instant may be negative                      | 1        |
| An unknown asset is read rather than refused    | 1        |
| Every batch of ticks starts a new recorder      | 2        |
| One recorder serves every asset                 | 2        |
| An asset with a record is provisioned again     | 1        |
| Zero days still provisions                      | 1        |

Two were uncaught on the first attempt, and both for one reason: every test fed
a whole tick stream in a single `observe` call, which is the one calling pattern
under which a per-call recorder and a shared recorder both work. The venue calls
`observe` once per scheduler tick — a handful of ticks — and flushes on the
checkpoint cadence, so almost every closed bar is accepted in one call and
drained in a much later one. There are now two tests in that shape: one comparing
1,800 ticks delivered in batches of seven against the same stream delivered at
once, and one interleaving two assets.
