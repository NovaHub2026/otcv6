## 1. The panel drew a candle the record does not hold

The live builder skipped only buckets up to the last **flushed** bar. A bucket
that had begun before the client connected but had not yet been flushed fell
between the two, and was rebuilt from whichever ticks arrived after connect.

Measured against the running service: a 22:03 candle opening at 68795.53 where
the record says 68825.00, and **missing the record's high of 68825.00** — a price
the extreme-preserving contract exists to protect, wrong for this viewer only.
The control bucket, fully covered by the stream, matched to the last bit.

A live bar is built only for a bucket that started after this client connected.
Until the next boundary the newest bar on the chart is the newest bar in the
record, which is the only honest thing to draw.

## 2. A market could stop for ever and the venue would say `ok`

`Venue.advance()` returns `advanceDetailed(now).published` and discards the
failures, and the service called only that. A market past its catch-up bound
refuses every later advance — `#lastAdvancedAt` moves only after the check — so
it stopped **permanently**, while `/health` returned `{"status":"ok"}`,
`/markets/:id` returned the frozen last price, and the panel showed a chart that
had stopped moving with the status `live`.

Failures are collected and logged once per distinct reason now, and `/health`
answers `degraded` with the asset and the reason. A health endpoint that cannot
report the one failure its process has is worse than none, because it is what a
monitor watches.

## 3. The documented refusal that could never be sent

`writeHead(200)` ran before `subscribe`, so `EvictedError` could not become a
status code: an evicted `?from=` produced 200 with a zero-length body and put the
reason in the server log, while the browser client reconnected with the same
evicted sequence for ever. The endpoint's own docstring says such a request is a
400 _"because a client cannot detect what it never received"_.

Nothing is written until the subscription exists; replay delivered during
`subscribe` is buffered and flushed after the headers.

`Last-Event-ID` is honoured too. The endpoint emits `id: <sequence>` on every
event — the mechanism a browser uses to resume — and read only `?from=`: after a
15-second disconnect a reconnect carrying the header skipped 19 ticks in silence.

## 4. Two stores, one guard

`backfillMarket` refused when a **state record** existed, while the damage
happens in the **history** store, and the two are written at different times:
candles flush throughout, the checkpoint once at the end. A crash between them
left history and no record, and the guard then admitted a re-run which either
died on the first append — leaving the asset permanently unprovisionable — or,
with a later genesis, spliced a second market into the same id: 5,070 minute rows
spanning two histories with a 2,130-minute hole and a 3,984-step price jump.

Both stores are consulted, and the refusal names the repair, because deleting an
asset's past is an operator's decision and there is no safe automatic one.

## 5. What an endpoint costs

`/markets/:id/history` had no window cap, no pagination, no rate limit and no
auth, and `node:sqlite`'s `.all()` is synchronous. One 90-day minute request
measured **20.5 MB of JSON and 1,496 ms of blocked event loop**; sixty concurrent
ones took the process from 100 MB to 1.86 GB. The venue survived only because
Node yields between handlers, which is the single thing separating that from the
finding above it.

Bounded at 20,000 bars — two orders of magnitude above anything the panel asks
for, whose largest view is ninety daily rows. The _availability_ refusal comes
first, deliberately: answering "too many bars" to a timeframe that is never
served would send the caller shortening a window that cannot work.

## 6. And a temporary file unique per process rather than per call

Two concurrent saves of one asset raced: the first `rename` moved the file the
second was still writing. Reproduced 200 of 200, and observed on ordinary SIGTERM
shutdowns — where the rejection aborted the checkpoint loop, so the _remaining_
markets got no final checkpoint either. `stop()` waits for an in-flight advance
now instead of checkpointing on top of one.
