## 1. Objective

Make the specification's sections I, H and M true of the product, on the sign
source PH-24.1 composes: an operator selects the **current or next candle**,
types a **price**, sees whether it is reachable and how hard, **applies** it,
watches the candle close there, and can **release** the market at any time —
with every act in the session record (§78) and the whole of it on the screen.

## 2. What "close" means, and where the window starts

**The close is the price in force at the candle's end, inclusive** (ADR-0017).
The target instant is `bucketEnd(now)` for the current candle or
`bucketEnd(now) + duration` for the next, on the chosen timeframe. The selection
window is every tick the engine will draw from now up to and including a tick
at that instant — `labStepsAhead` already keeps a tick whose instant equals the
window's end.

**The window starts after the pending tick.** A hosted market holds one drawn,
unpublished tick, and `snapshotEngine()` is taken after it was drawn: a fork's
first `next()` is the tick _after_ pending. So the distance to close is measured
from the pending tick's price, and the armed vector begins with the draw that
follows it. Getting this wrong by one tick is the difference between an exact
close and a close one step away, and a test pins it.

**Compute and arm in one critical section.** Between reading the fork's steps
and arming the vector, the venue must not advance — or the steps describe a
future that has partly happened. `VenueService.betweenAdvances(fn)` awaits the
in-flight advance and runs `fn` synchronously (fork, select, arm: under five
milliseconds, PH-23.1 §3). It is economically blind and Lab-agnostic: it knows
nothing about what `fn` does.

## 3. A typed price, on a log lattice

The lattice is the canonical tick (ADR-0004): a log quantum, not a decimal
step, so not every decimal is a level. `fromDisplayPrice` gives the exact
(fractional) level; it is rounded to the nearest, and if that level's display
price at the asset's precision is not the string the operator typed, the
request is answered with **the two neighbouring lattice prices** and nothing is
armed. Silent rounding would make "Close = 1.085100" a close at 1.085099, which
is the one thing §I5 forbids.

Parity halves the reachable lattice (PH-23.1 §5). A target unreachable by parity
is answered with the two reachable neighbours, ±1 level, by name.

## 4. Routes

| Route                                | Does                                                                                                                              |
| ------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `GET /lab/markets/:id/close/preview` | `?price=&bucket=current\|next&timeframe=` → target level, instant, ticks in window, measured reachability, or the neighbours (§3) |
| `POST /lab/markets/:id/close`        | same parameters → selects and **arms**; returns what preview returns plus `armed: true` and the session entry                     |
| `POST /lab/markets/:id/release`      | releases the sign source; returns how many scripted signs were discarded                                                          |
| `GET /lab/markets/:id/control`       | `armed`, `remaining`, the target if any                                                                                           |
| `GET /lab/session`                   | `LabSession.timelines()` — engine events and Lab actions, two arrays, never merged (§72–§73)                                      |

`LabSession` is provided by `LabModule` and fed by every route above with §78's
fields: timestamp, asset, engine version, action, parameters, initial state
(pending price, sequence, cursors), resulting state, outcome, diagnostics
(acceptance rate, attempts, ticks in window).

## 5. The screen

The Lab's `CANDLE CLOSE CONTROL` section becomes the control: timeframe and
bucket, a price field in display units with the asset's precision, a live
preview (reachability band, rate, ticks remaining, time remaining), **Apply**
and **Release**, the armed state, and — beneath — the session's two timelines.
The banner stays. The visible half lands in this subphase, because building it
is how PH-23.5 found three defects the API hid.

## 6. What this subphase may not do

- Exist outside `LabModule` (ADR-0015 §3; `composition.test.ts` and
  `labSurface.test.ts`).
- Round a typed price silently, or accept one that is not a lattice level.
- Report "applied" for a target the market cannot reach: an unreachable close is
  refused with its reason and nothing is armed.
- Merge the two timelines.

## 7. Acceptance criteria

1. On a hosted Lab market, a reachable close applied to the current candle is
   the price in force at the candle's end, exactly, on the engine's own steps —
   verified by advancing the venue through the candle and reading the record.
2. The same for the next candle.
3. A typed price that is not a lattice level is refused with its two neighbours;
   a parity-unreachable level is refused with its two reachable neighbours.
4. Distance is measured from the pending tick and the vector begins after it —
   a test that arms one tick early or late fails.
5. Release mid-window returns the market to the keystream with no jump: the next
   tick is the keystream's own.
6. Every apply and release appears in the Lab timeline with §78's fields and in
   the engine timeline never.
7. The screen does all of it in Chromium, with the banner, and the browser suite
   asserts the applied close on the drawn candle.
