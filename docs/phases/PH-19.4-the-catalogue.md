## 1. The finding that would have stopped the product

`alt-crypto` draws a tail-weight target in [130, 165] and a cascade depth in
[5, 8]. At depth 5 the cascade cannot supply that much inflation however wide its
components are, so 3.64% of its briefs could not be authored at all. P(a
twelve-asset draw hits one) is **36%**: better than one build in three of the
hundred-asset catalogue this whole line of work exists for, failing outright.

The acceptance survived only because it is deterministically seeded and its first
three `alt-crypto` draws happened to be feasible. Any change to the sampler's
draw order would have re-rolled that.

Sampling and authoring were two steps with nothing between them. The target is
clamped now to what the rhythm just drawn can reach — the way `cascadeSpacing`
already was — computed from that personality with `clustering` at its own upper
bound, which is what the solve would be searching against. Measured after: **0
unauthorable in 500 alt-crypto draws** with the clamp engaging on 15.4% of them,
and 0 in 960 across all eight archetypes.

The regression pins the personality that proved it rather than searching for one:
a depth-5 draw whose cascade tops out near 128 against a band asking for 130.

## 2. Amplitude is not character

The differentiation guard refused only when two personalities were close **and**
their quarterly dispersions were within 5%. So an exact copy registered at 1.06×
the budget was admitted — and the project's own classifier puts such a pair at
46.3 / 47.5 / 51.2% on the **full** signature against a 50% chance rate. One
market, louder, registered twice.

`volatility` is out of the distance and the exemption is gone. Scale is a
_budget_, drawn per asset from its family's band and deliberately free to vary;
character is what INV-007 is about. Two assets differing only in scale are not
two personalities, at any ratio.

The threshold moved with it and was re-measured from **forty** independent
96-asset catalogues rather than one: their closest pair ranges 0.0157 to 0.0245,
so 0.02 would refuse a legitimate draw in five of the forty and 0.01 in none —
while a copy sits at zero.

## 3. `alt-crypto` was a template with jitter

Its siblings were not distinguishable from three clones of one personality: a
4.7pp lift at p ≈ 0.17, against 8–20pp for every other archetype. It was the
narrowest box in the catalogue, and a region that small is not a region. Widened
on the five traits that were tightest, keeping the character — fast, shallow,
extreme — and the feasible spacing corner.

`blue-chip-index` had the opposite problem at its slow end: at 7,000 ms and 38%
branching more than 1% of 30-second horizons hold no tick, so the 1% quantile is
exactly zero and the refusal reads "the asset does not move", blaming amplitude
for a tempo problem.

## 4. The acceptance measured the wrong statistic

`min(real) > max(control) + 5pp` over three seeds is a comparison of two extremes
over a small sample. An auditor measured it failing on **26 of 120** seed triples
drawn from ten stream families, while the underlying distributions separate by
eleven points of mean.

Six seeds now, compared **pairwise** — each family's sampled figure against its
own control, which is what the design supports — plus the mean lift. Measured:
16.6, 14.3, 13.1, 14.2, 11.4, 12.4 points, every seed.

The acceptance also registered at one calibration replicate, so one personality
registered three times produced published lattices spanning 2.4×. Three
replicates now: the quantum is a 1% quantile of a heavy-tailed variable, and
combining three by median is what `CALIBRATION_REPLICATES` exists for.

## 5. And the fix uncovered a latent overflow

`pooled.push(...returns)` spreads a whole replicate onto the argument stack.
Raising the turnover floor made spans long enough to hit it: 32 turnovers of a
46-hour cascade is 61 simulated days and 176,000 windows, and a
`RangeError: Maximum call stack size exceeded` from a line that reads like a copy.
