## 1. Objective

Make a candle close at an exact requested price, without putting a mechanism in
this repository that reads a desired outcome and moves the market.

This is first because it is the only part of the Lab whose feasibility was not
obvious, and because everything else in PH-23 is a view onto it.

## 2. The theorem supplies the method

ADR-0003: an increment is `sign × magnitude`, the sign is an **independent fair
coin**, and the magnitude engine is structurally unable to observe a sign, a
price, or anything derived from them. Two consequences follow.

**Every sign sequence is equally the engine's own.** There is no "correct" one
it would have produced. The coin is fair, so all `2^n` of them are its output.

**The magnitudes do not depend on the signs.** So the Lab does not run the
engine many times: it runs it **once**, takes the step sizes, and samples sign
vectors until one lands on the target.

That second claim is the load-bearing one, and it is verified against the real
engine rather than assumed — `stepIndependence.test.ts` runs three different
sign scripts through the shipped factory and asserts the step and interval
sequences are identical over three hundred ticks. It is also, in a different
form, what the mirror test asserts on every gate.

## 3. Measured

Running the engine through a one-minute bucket, then selecting a close one
standard deviation away:

| asset  | ticks in 60 s | sigma (steps) | attempts | selection  | engine run | reachability |
| ------ | ------------- | ------------- | -------- | ---------- | ---------- | ------------ |
| eurusd | 26            | 88            | 13       | **0.4 ms** | 3.2 ms     | easy         |
| gbpjpy | 68            | 213           | 89       | **1.0 ms** | 1.6 ms     | easy         |
| btcusd | 177           | 230           | 100      | **2.0 ms** | 2.4 ms     | easy         |
| spx    | 15            | 372           | 232      | **0.3 ms** | 0.6 ms     | normal       |
| xauusd | 14            | 69            | 175      | **0.2 ms** | 0.3 ms     | normal       |

**Two milliseconds at worst, and the engine run dominates it.**

The estimate written into PH-23's phase document was 0.11 s — pessimistic by
two orders of magnitude, because it assumed re-running the engine per trial. The
theorem makes that unnecessary, and it is worth recording that the correction
went in the useful direction for the wrong reason: the arithmetic was right
about the _number of trials_ and wrong about what a trial costs.

## 4. What comes free, by construction

None of these is enforced by the implementation; they are properties of drawing
a real path:

| Specification                                              | Why it holds                              |
| ---------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------- |
| §21 the constraint is terminal, not a path constraint      | nothing constrains any tick but the sum   |
| §22 no straight-line interpolation                         | there is no interpolation                 |
| §25 natural microstructure                                 | it _is_ the microstructure                |
| §29 overshoot is allowed                                   | as often as chance says; asserted         |
| §31 valid OHLC                                             | the path is a real path                   |
| §32 free wicks, diverse bodies                             | signs are drawn, not shaped               |
| §28 no fixed last-seconds pattern                          | there is no convergence mechanism to time |
| §70 no terminal-convergence signature                      | there is no signature to find             |
| §71 controlled candles indistinguishable from natural ones | they _are_ natural ones                   |

**Rejection sampling is deliberate.** An accepted vector is drawn uniformly from
the sign vectors that hit the target, which is the most natural possible path to
that close. A constructive solver would be faster and would produce a
recognisable shape — and a recognisable shape is precisely what §28 and §71
exist to forbid. The guard that catches this plants exactly that solver.

## 5. Reachability is measured (§36)

The sampler's acceptance rate **is** the answer §36 asks for, so Easy / Normal /
Difficult / Critical / Outside Natural Range stop being a heuristic and become
bands over a measured probability.

Two impossibilities are reported without sampling at all, because exhaustion is
a poor way to learn them:

- **Parity.** `sum(±steps)` always shares a parity with `sum(steps)`, so half of
  every lattice is unreachable however long one samples.
- **Range.** No assignment moves further than `sum(steps)`.

## 6. Acceptance

1. **Met.** A reachable target is closed on exactly, with the engine's own steps
   in their own order.
2. **Met.** The same target on a different draw gives a different path.
3. **Met.** The path crosses its own close before settling there — a path that
   never did would be the straight line §22 forbids.
4. **Met.** Parity and range impossibilities are named without sampling.
5. **Met.** Reachability is the measured acceptance rate.
6. **Met.** Step independence verified on the shipped engine, not assumed.

## 7. Approval

**APPROVED** 2026-09-03. Three plants, three catches: steering the steps toward
the target, dropping the parity check, and replacing the sampler with a
constructive solver. The last one failed four guards at once, which is the
correct reaction to a mechanism that would have worked and been detectable.

Nothing in the price path changed. `closeSelection.ts` reads step sizes and
returns signs; it never touches the engine.
