## 1. What was missing

`apps/web/src/panel.stat.test.ts` builds the panel from source, boots the real
service on an OS-assigned port with two backfilled days, starts `next start` on
another, and drives Chromium against it. Four tests, 33 seconds.

It asserts what `fetch` from Node structurally cannot observe: that no request is
blocked, that the canvas has a **size**, that bars are drawn, that the displayed
price moves, that switching an asset switches the market, and that a coarser
timeframe holds fewer bars — with no console error and no failed request.

The build runs inside `beforeAll`. `next start` serves whatever `.next` holds, so
a stale directory would let this suite pass a panel that is no longer in the
tree, which is the same class of failure it exists to end.

## 2. The plants, and the two that got through

Six defects were planted and the suite re-run against each. **This section
exists because two of them passed**, and what they revealed is worth more than
the four that failed on the first attempt.

| Planted defect                                       | Result                     |
| ---------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------- |
| chart container sizes to its content (no `inset: 0`) | caught — 3 of 4 tests fail |
| the engine is not proxied under the panel's origin   | caught — 4 of 4 tests fail |
| the live price is frozen                             | **passed**, then caught    |
| the stream stays subscribed to the first asset       | **passed**, then caught    |
| the chart is keyed on display precision and reused   | **passed**, then caught    |
| history is fetched for whichever asset was first     | **passed**, then caught    |

**A clock inside the assertion.** "The price moves" read the `last-price`
element, which held the price _and_ the time of the tick in one text node. The
text changed every second whatever the price did. The price and the clock are
now separate elements — a test-interface decision, recorded in the component.

**A readout of the props is a test of the props.** The asset-switch test compared
the `quantum` readout across a switch. The quantum is a React prop; it re-renders
whether or not the chart followed. A chart planted to be reused across assets
passed it.

**And the price cannot identify a market.** The second attempt compared the
displayed price against the asset's reference. It passed against a stream planted
to stay on `eurusd`, and against history planted to fetch `eurusd` always,
because `displayPrice` converts an integer count through _the selected asset's_
quantum and reference (ADR-0004). Ticks from the wrong market land near the right
price. **The number on screen genuinely cannot tell you whose market it is** —
which is a fact about this design worth knowing beyond this test.

What can is the traffic. Each selected asset must be asked for by name at both
tiers — stored candles and live stream — and nothing may be asked of another
market afterwards. All four survivors fail against that.

## 3. What this cost the panel, and what it did not

Five `data-testid` handles. The first version of the suite selected the live
price by its hex colour; a panel testable only by matching a theme is a panel
whose tests break when someone changes one.

Nothing in `packages/` moved. The panel is outside the replayable set by design
(`docs/architecture/CATALOGUE_AND_PANEL.md` §5) and this subphase does not change
that.

## 4. CI

`.github/workflows/ci.yml` installs Chromium with its system libraries and sets
`OTC_REQUIRE_BROWSER=1` on the statistical job. Locally, without root, the suite
**skips loudly** and names the packages that fix it; in CI a missing browser is a
failure. A coverage layer that can silently disappear is CA6-10 with a longer
fuse.
