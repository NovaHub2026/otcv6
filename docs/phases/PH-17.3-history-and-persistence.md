# PH-17.3 — Backdated History and Continuous Persistence at Scale

Type: SUBPHASE TECHNICAL DOCUMENT
Identifier: PH-17.3
Parent: [PH-17](PH-17-assets-become-data.md)
Status: APPROVED
Created: 2026-09-01

## 1. The requirement

An asset created today must have ninety days of chart on the first frame an
observer sees, and must go on recording from there.

## 2. A backfilled market is the market

The process is deterministic given a keyring, a genesis instant and a genesis
price. So a market whose genesis is ninety days ago and which has been advanced
to now is not a simulation of history — it _is_ the history, generated once,
recorded once, and continued live from the same latent state.

The obvious implementation drives the engine directly and skips the runtime.
That would be a second way of producing prices, and the property the product
rests on is that there is exactly one (INV-003); any divergence would surface as
a chart that disagrees with the record precisely where the backfill ends.

So `backfillMarket` uses `HostedMarket.advanceTo` in steps no larger than the
catch-up bound, against a clock that moves faster than wall time. Every tick is
published by the code that will publish the next one.

`backfill.test.ts` asserts the identity against a market advanced **every
second** rather than every fifteen — 21,600 calls against 1,440, one stream —
because a comparison at the same cadence would be the same code called the same
number of times, and what has to be true is that the tick stream does not depend
on how often anything asks for it (INV-002).

## 3. A backfill is genesis, and refuses to be anything else

`backfillMarket` refuses if a record already exists. Generating a second history
under one id would publish a different market to observers who saw the first
(INV-002), spend keystream positions twice (INV-010), and leave every settlement
recorded against the old series unreproducible (INV-009).

It also closes a subtler door. A backfill that could be repeated could be
repeated **until the chart looked right**, and then the operator would be
choosing the prices — economic blindness (INV-001) broken not in the generator
but in the provisioning around it. The history an asset gets is the first one it
was given.

The step is bounded by the same fifteen seconds ADR-0010 gives a running venue.
A backfill has no more authority to invent an unobserved interval than a live
market does, and a larger step would also stop it being the live path, which is
the only reason to trust its output.

## 4. History is candles, and that is a measurement

[`CYCLE-6-BACKFILL-SCALE.md`](../evidence/CYCLE-6-BACKFILL-SCALE.md) measured
ninety days for each of the five assets:

| Asset  | ticks      | minute bars | hourly bars | seconds |
| ------ | ---------- | ----------- | ----------- | ------- |
| spx    | 2,302,010  | 129,599     | 2,160       | 7.1     |
| xauusd | 3,877,620  | 129,599     | 2,160       | 11.6    |
| eurusd | 5,541,871  | 129,599     | 2,160       | 15.7    |
| gbpjpy | 10,674,532 | 129,599     | 2,160       | 21.2    |
| btcusd | 22,782,030 | 129,599     | 2,160       | 59.0    |

**The candle count is fixed by the calendar, not by the asset.** `btcusd`
generates ten times the ticks of `spx` and stores exactly the same number of
bars. That is what makes a hundred-asset catalogue affordable, and it is why the
tick record keeps only what a settlement can still be disputed against while the
candle history keeps everything.

Two stored tiers, one minute and one hour, because every timeframe the product
offers nests into one of them and each additional tier is another thing that can
disagree with the ticks. A daily chart of a quarter reads ninety rows from the
hourly tier instead of 129,600 from the minute one.

Two things are deliberately not offered:

- **Anything finer than a minute, historically.** `readTimeframe` refuses rather
  than returning a coarser series under the requested name. INV-004 says the
  displayed timeframe never changes the market; it does not say every timeframe
  is available over every span, and pretending otherwise would put a shape on the
  screen that no tick produced.
- **Open candles.** A bar still accumulating has a high and a low that are not
  yet true, and once stored nothing can tell the difference. The hourly tier
  therefore emits a bar only when every minute inside it has closed, rather than
  when the caller happens to drain.

## 5. Every guard watched failing

Thirteen defects planted, each reverted immediately.

| Planted defect                                       | Caught by                    | Failures |
| ---------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------- | -------- |
| The recorder emits the hour it is still accumulating | `history.test.ts`            | 12       |
| The recorder forgets an unfinished hour's minutes    | `history.test.ts`            | 4        |
| The recorder drops what closed before the batch      | `history.test.ts`            | 1        |
| Every read comes from the coarse tier                | `history.test.ts`            | 4        |
| The finer-than-base guard is inverted                | `history.test.ts`            | 8        |
| A refused batch leaves what it already wrote         | `historyConformance.test.ts` | 1        |
| The disk store appends without a transaction         | `historyConformance.test.ts` | 9        |
| A read window includes its own end                   | `historyConformance.test.ts` | 1        |
| A backfill may run twice                             | `backfill.test.ts`           | 1        |
| A backfill may step past the catch-up bound          | `backfill.test.ts`           | 1        |
| A backfill leaves no checkpoint                      | `backfill.test.ts`           | 4        |
| A backfill retains every tick it generated           | `backfill.test.ts`           | 1        |
| A backfill stops a step short of its target          | `backfill.test.ts`           | 3        |

Three were uncaught on the first attempt:

- **The recorder drops what closed before the batch.** Every test accepted and
  then drained immediately, which is the one calling pattern the defect survives.
  Real callers accept every simulated step and drain every few thousand, so the
  defect would have lost all but the last batch of history. There is now a test
  in the shape callers actually use.
- **A backfill may step past the catch-up bound.** The assertion matched the
  error _message_, and `HostedMarket` refuses an over-long advance with a
  `CatchUpTooLargeError` whose text also says "catch-up bound" — the right answer
  for the wrong reason. It asserts the error type now.
- **A backfill stops a step short.** There was no assertion that the backfill
  reached its target at all; the ticks agreed, the candles covered almost the
  whole span, and the checkpoint resumed cleanly into a market silently fifteen
  seconds late. The first replacement measured the gap in mean tick intervals and
  failed on ordinary arrival variance at 7,077 ms against 6,705. The exact
  statement was available all along: the market holds one tick pulled ahead and
  not yet due, so a backfill that reached its target has a **pending tick beyond
  it**.

## 6. The disk implementation

`SqliteCandleHistory`, in its own database rather than beside the state store.
They have opposite shapes — a handful of rows rewritten constantly against
millions written once and never touched — and the second is the one an operator
will want to move, archive, or serve from a replica.

`historyConformance.test.ts` runs one battery against both implementations, the
shape PH-14 established for the lease store. The failures that matter are the
ones where they differ: a `WITHOUT ROWID` key that reorders, an integer column
that widens a branded price, a partial batch left behind by a failed append.
None can be found by testing either alone, and the transaction plant proved it —
removing `BEGIN IMMEDIATE` failed nine assertions on disk and none in memory.
