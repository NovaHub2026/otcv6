## 1. Why this comes before the lattice

PH-37 chooses each asset's lattice by measuring what it refunds. That is only
possible if changing the lattice does not change the market — and it did. The
arrival process was excited by `previousMagnitude`, and the engine set that to
`steps`, the **floored lattice count**. A magnitude below one quantum rounds to
zero; a zero excites nothing and does not enter the running average the
excitation normalises against. So a coarser lattice starved the process:

|                         |   ×1 |  ×8 |           ×32 |
| ----------------------- | ---: | --: | ------------: |
| EUR/USD, ticks a second | 1.00 |   — | 0.74 (at ×27) |
| BNB, ticks a second     | 3.67 |   — |          1.52 |

Every candidate lattice was a different market, so no measurement across them
could have meant anything.

## 2. What is built

**The layers above see the magnitude** (`engine.ts`): `previousMagnitude` is
now `magnitude / logQuantum` before the rounding. Both consumers — Hawkes and
the structure layer — normalise against their own running average, so the unit
cancels and only the zeros and the discreteness were ever doing anything. It is
still an absolute size, so nothing here sees a sign (ADR-0003).

**Excitation opens at its stationary value** (`hawkes.ts`): `n / (1 − n)`, which
`branchingRatio`'s own docstring already names as the reason the realised mean
interval is `baseIntervalMs · (1 − n)`. It opened at zero, so every market began
at the immigrant rate and climbed.

**The magnitude average is seeded by the first magnitude**, not by the absolute
`referenceMagnitude`. Seeding the excitation exposed it: the same seed left
EUR/USD 24% slow on its own lattice and 60% fast on one 32 times coarser.

**`REGIME_ACTIVITY_SHARE` 0.5 → 0.25**, so a regime arrives as a bigger step
rather than almost entirely as more ticks.

## 4. Two claims the measurements refuted

- **The units coupling is not the root of PH-34's pace error.** With it gone the
  formula `baseIntervalMs · (1 − n)` still misses by ×0.94 to ×2.93. The cause
  is the intensity clamp: the stationary multiplier `1/(1 − n)` is 2.14 on TSLA,
  2.32 on EUR/USD, 3.60 on BTC and 7.68 on DOGE against a clamp of 8, and the
  pace error follows that order exactly. The clamp is deliberate, so PH-34's
  measured fit loop is the right mechanism and stays.
- **The step ratio at a quarter is ×1.68, not the ×1.83 first written.** The
  step ratio is `span^(1 − share)`; the duration coupling cancels out of it.

## 5. What it costs

Every pinned tick rate in the catalogue moves, which PH-37.2 recalibrates
anyway. A market that seams now opens at its settled pace instead of half of it
— visible to a broker as a market that does not slow down after a restart.
